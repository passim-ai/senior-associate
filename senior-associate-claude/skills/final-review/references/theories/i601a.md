# I-601A provisional unlawful presence waiver packet

Officer theories that most often drive an RFE, NOID, or denial in this case type. Read them with `../checklists.md`, which has the checks for every packet (and the theories for any I-485). The authorities files carry the citations; confirm a theory against current authority before you rely on it.

- A rejection on its face: no State Department immigrant visa fee receipt showing payment (unless DV), no petition approval or DV printout, a Part 3 basis or case number left blank, or the I-601A sent with another form (I-601A instructions, "Processing Information").
- Hardship argued to the applicant or the children without tying it to the U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent, or a parent or child named as the qualifying relative when only a spouse or parent counts.
- Hardship that is the common consequence of separation or relocation (family separation, lower income, readjusting) with nothing beyond it, or claims with no records behind them (9 USCIS-PM B.5(B); *Matter of Pilch*, 21 I&N Dec. 627, 633 (BIA 1996)).
- One scenario argued (relocation or separation) with no sworn statement from the qualifying relative that it is the one that would happen, so the officer weighs the other (9 USCIS-PM B.4(B)).
- A second ground of inadmissibility in the record (a reentry after unlawful presence or removal, a misrepresentation, a conviction): the consular officer would refuse the visa and the waiver is revoked.
- Removal proceedings not administratively closed, a final order without an approved I-212, or an unexpired voluntary departure.
- Unfavorable discretionary factors (arrests, unauthorized work, prior violations) left unexplained.
