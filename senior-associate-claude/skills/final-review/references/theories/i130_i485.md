# I-130/I-485 immediate relative packet

Officer theories that most often drive an RFE, NOID, or denial in this case type. Read them with `../checklists.md`, which has the checks for every packet (and the theories for any I-485). The authorities files carry the citations; confirm a theory against current authority before you rely on it.

## The marriage

- Thin bona fides: few joint records, records clustered just before filing, no commingling, or separate addresses without an explanation.
- A marital history that differs between the I-130, the I-130A, the I-485, and the civil documents. Inconsistencies must be resolved "by independent objective evidence" (*Matter of Ho*, 19 I&N Dec. 582, 591-92 (BIA 1988)).
- A prior marriage not shown to have legally ended before this one.
- A marriage entered while in proceedings (INA 204(g), 245(e)) without clear and convincing evidence of bona fides (*Matter of Arthur*, 20 I&N Dec. 475, 479 (BIA 1992)).
- Section 204(c) concerns about prior marriage fraud in the record: the officer needs substantial and probative evidence, "more than probably true" (*Matter of P. Singh*, 27 I&N Dec. 598, 607 (BIA 2019)), so look at what's in the beneficiary's file, not the officer's hunch.
- A permanent-resident petitioner who got status through an earlier marriage less than five years ago, without clear and convincing evidence about that marriage (INA 204(a)(2)(A)).
- A citizen petitioner with a conviction for a specified offense against a minor (the Adam Walsh Act, INA 204(a)(1)(A)(viii)); read the parallel rule in INA 204(a)(1)(B) before applying it to a permanent-resident petitioner.
