# I-751 Joint Petition to Remove Conditions

`matter_type`: I-751 joint petition to remove conditions (`i751_joint`). Source: I-751 instructions, ed. 04/01/24 (uscis.gov/i-751, checked 2026-10-29).

Common required items (re-checked line by line against the instructions on 2026-10-29):

- A copy of the front and back of the conditional resident card, and of each included conditional resident child's card.
- Filed jointly "during the 90-day period immediately before your conditional residence expires", with both spouses' handwritten signatures. A late joint petition needs a written explanation showing the delay was due to extraordinary circumstances beyond the couple's control and its length was reasonable.
- Evidence that the marriage was entered in good faith, "to demonstrate the circumstances of the relationship from the date of the marriage to the present date, and to demonstrate any circumstances surrounding the end of the relationship, if it has ended". The instructions' examples: children's birth certificates; a lease or mortgage showing joint occupancy or ownership; joint financial records (accounts with transaction history, complete joint tax returns, insurance naming the other spouse, joint utility bills or loans); other relevant documents. Evidence covering only recent months is `partial`.
- Criminal history: "Criminal records" in the common rules in `../evidence-checklists.md`.

Suggested bona fide evidence:

- Affidavits are one of the instructions' examples, not a requirement. When used: sworn or affirmed by at least two people who have known both spouses since conditional residence was granted and have personal knowledge of the marriage, each giving the affiant's full name and address, date and place of birth, relationship to the couple, and how they know; originals; and supported by other evidence (the instructions, p. 5).
- Joint residence records across the conditional period.
- Joint financial accounts, liabilities, taxes, insurance, utilities, and benefits.
- Children's birth certificates and school or medical records showing both parents.
- Photos, travel, family events, and communications.
