# I-140 National Interest Waiver (EB-2 NIW)

`matter_type`: I-140 national interest waiver petition packet (`i140_niw`). Sources: I-140 instructions, ed. 06/07/24; uscis.gov/i-140 (updated 07/24/2026); USCIS's EB-2 page and its NIW filing tips (updated 01/15/2025); USCIS's I-140 initial evidence checklist (updated 11/06/2024); 6 USCIS-PM F.5 (current as of 2026-10-27); all checked 2026-10-30. The petition is Part 2 box 1.h, usually a self-petition (Part 5 item 1, Self), though an employer may file one. It goes alone, or with an I-485 when a visa is available to the beneficiary (an `attorney_decision`, from the month's Visa Bulletin and the chart USCIS uses); with the I-485, add the I-485 items in `i140_i485.md`, except Supplement J (never for an NIW) and the I-864 (only when a relative filed the I-140 or owns 5% or more of the employer that did, so never for a self-petition).

- **The labor certification portion (blocks filing):** a completed Form ETA-9089 Appendix A and the ETA-9089 Final Determination, which "must be signed by the employer/petitioner, the attorney/agent (if applicable), and the beneficiary/self-petitioner" (I-140 checklist; the EB-2 page says the petition "must be accompanied by" both). These are Department of Labor forms the forms tools don't fill: an `attorney_action` to prepare them from the fact table, with the same endeavor, job title and SOC code as the I-140. The Policy Manual still accepts an ETA 750B, which DOL no longer uses; the 9089 pair satisfies both.
- **EB-2 eligibility, by one of two routes (required):**
  - *Advanced degree:* the official academic record (diploma and transcripts) for a U.S. degree above a bachelor's, or a foreign degree with a credential evaluation showing its U.S. equivalent. Or a bachelor's (or foreign equivalent) plus letters from current or former employers showing five years of progressive experience in the specialty, all of it after the degree and related to the degree's field or the endeavor (EB-2 filing tips). Each letter gives the writer's name, address and title and a specific description of the duties (8 C.F.R. 204.5(g)(1)); a letter without them is `partial`. The occupation through which the endeavor is pursued must be a profession, one that needs at least a bachelor's for entry, and "the occupation underlying the endeavor is determinative" (6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(1)).
  - *Exceptional ability:* at least three of the six: an academic record in the area; letters showing ten years of full-time experience in the occupation; a license or certification; pay that shows exceptional ability; membership in professional associations; and recognition for achievements and significant contributions by peers, government or professional bodies (comparable evidence when these don't fit the occupation). Meeting three is only the first step: the record as a whole must show expertise significantly above the ordinary, in an area "directly related to the person's proposed endeavor".
  - A three-year foreign degree, or a bachelor's equivalency built from several credentials, is a `risk` item and an `attorney_decision` (the authorities file, "Adverse or limiting authority").
- **The proposed endeavor (required):** a detailed statement, in the petitioner's own words, of the specific endeavor: what they will do, the projects and goals, where, how (at a named employer or through their own company), and on what timeline. It is "more specific than the general occupation". An entrepreneur adds the business plan, the company's formation and ownership records, funding, and contracts or letters of intent. The endeavor must read the same in the statement, the letter, I-140 Part 6 and the ETA-9089 Appendix A: eligibility is fixed at filing, and an endeavor redefined in an RFE response invites a material-change finding.
- **Prong 1, substantial merit and national importance:** evidence of the endeavor's potential impact beyond the petitioner's employer or clients ("broader implications, such as for a field, a region, or the public at large"): government reports, national strategies or agency priorities that name the problem; the federal critical and emerging technologies list for a STEM endeavor; data on the field; independent coverage of the petitioner's work; evidence of interest in adopting or licensing it. A shortage in the occupation, or the field's general importance, isn't enough by itself.
- **Prong 2, well positioned:** the CV; publications with citation records (a Google Scholar or Scopus printout); patents; grants and funding; awards; media coverage; contracts, letters of intent, users, customers or investors; evidence of progress toward the endeavor; and recommendation letters from experts with first-hand knowledge that give specific examples. Some letters should come from independent experts who know the work but not the petitioner personally, and each claim in a letter needs corroboration elsewhere in the record. A STEM Ph.D. tied to the endeavor is "an especially positive factor". A business plan helps but "should be supported by other independent evidence".
- **Prong 3, on balance:** why a job offer or labor certification is impractical (self-employment, an entrepreneur, or unique knowledge a labor certification can't capture), the benefit to the United States even if U.S. workers are available, any urgency, potential job creation or economic impact, and any letter from an interested U.S. government agency or quasi-governmental body (a federally funded research center).
- **The beneficiary:** the passport's biographic page; for someone in the United States, the current I-94 and status documents (Part 3, and the Part 4 choice between adjustment and a visa abroad); and any earlier I-140 or other immigrant petition (Part 4 items 6-8).
- **The family (Part 7):** the spouse and every child, listed whether or not they will immigrate; marriage and birth certificates for those who will, for their later I-485s or visas.
- **No ability to pay:** USCIS's checklist asks for it for E21 petitions "except for national interest waivers".
- **Fee and filing:** $715 on paper or $665 online, plus the Asylum Program Fee: $300 for a self-petitioner (or small employer), $600 for other employers, $0 for a nonprofit. Every petitioner answers Part 1 items 5 and 6 or USCIS rejects the petition, and a self-petitioner answers No to item 5 and Yes to item 6. Premium processing is an I-907 ($2,965, paid separately; 45 business days), and an I-140 filed with one goes by mail (run `fees.py`). The priority date is the day USCIS receives the complete, signed petition with the fee, so a missing item delays it.
- **Translations:** foreign degree records, transcripts and employer letters most often need them.
