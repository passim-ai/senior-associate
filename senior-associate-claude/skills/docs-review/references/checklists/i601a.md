# I-601A Provisional Unlawful Presence Waiver

`matter_type`: I-601A provisional unlawful presence waiver packet (`i601a`). Source: I-601A instructions, ed. 01/20/25 (uscis.gov/i-601a, page updated 06/01/2026, checked 2026-10-30). The I-601A is filed on paper, alone: USCIS rejects it when it comes with an I-485, I-130, I-212, I-131 or I-765 (a G-1145 may go with it).

- **The immigrant visa case:** the I-797 approving the I-130, I-140 or I-360 (or, for a Diversity Visa selectee or derivative, the DV Entrant Status Check printout), and the State Department's immigrant visa processing fee receipt, placed on top of the I-601A. USCIS rejects a non-DV I-601A without proof the fee was paid; the form page says an "In Process" status, a bill or an adjustment fee receipt isn't enough. Either missing is `missing` and blocks filing.
- **Eligibility screen:** physically in the United States and 17 or older; no I-485 pending; not in removal proceedings unless they were administratively closed and not put back on the calendar (the closure order); no final order of removal unless an I-212 is already approved (its I-797); no reinstated order (an I-871 final decision). The instructions also list an unexpired grant of voluntary departure, which the current regulation (8 C.F.R. 212.7(e)(4), checked 2026-10-30) doesn't: an `attorney_decision` when it applies. Each fact the record doesn't settle is a `client_question`. (The instructions' rejection list still mentions a Part 3 item 5 question about DOS acting before January 3, 2013; the 01/20/25 form has no such item.)
- **Only unlawful presence:** the waiver covers the 3- and 10-year unlawful presence bars and nothing else, and it is revoked if the consular officer finds another ground. List every entry, departure, arrest, removal, and immigration encounter for the attorney; a `risk` item for anything that may be another ground (a reentry after a year of unlawful presence or after removal, a misrepresentation, criminal history).
- **The qualifying relative** is a U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse or parent (a child doesn't qualify). When that relative is the family petitioner, the relationship and status evidence aren't needed again. Otherwise ask for the marriage certificate and proof prior marriages ended, or the birth certificate (with the instructions' extra proof for a father, step-parent or adoptive parent), and the relative's citizenship (birth certificate, naturalization or citizenship certificate, passport, FS-240) or green card, front and back.
- **Admission or parole:** when the form says the applicant was admitted or paroled, the I-94 or the passport stamp; without it USCIS presumes entry without inspection.
- **Extreme hardship (the heart of the case):** the qualifying relative's signed statement, and records for each factor the instructions list: health (medical records and treating providers' letters), finances (tax returns, pay records, the monthly budget, debts), education, family ties and caregiving, and country conditions (including the State Department travel advisory for the country). Hardship under one scenario is enough, the relative staying in the United States without the applicant or relocating, when the relative's sworn statement says which will happen (9 USCIS-PM B.4(B), current as of 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-30): ask for that statement and the evidence for that scenario, and for both when the family hasn't decided. A psychological evaluation is common and is the attorney's call. Hardship to the applicant or the children counts only through its effect on the qualifying relative.
- **Discretion:** favorable factors (family ties, years in the United States, tax filings, steady work, community ties, rehabilitation) and the record on unfavorable ones (certified dispositions and police clearances for any arrest, unauthorized employment, prior immigration violations).
- **Fee:** $795; none for a VAWA self-petitioner or a Special Immigrant Juvenile (run `fees.py`). USCIS schedules biometrics after filing.
