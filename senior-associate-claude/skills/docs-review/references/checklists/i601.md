# I-601 Waiver of Grounds of Inadmissibility

`matter_type`: I-601 waiver of inadmissibility packet (`i601`). Source: I-601 instructions, ed. 01/20/25 (uscis.gov/i-601, page updated 10/07/2026, checked 2026-10-30). The I-601 is filed on paper. Where it goes depends on the situation (`filing_locations.py --form I-601`): with an I-485, wherever the I-485 goes; with a pending I-485, a lockbox chosen by the receipt number; after a consular officer's finding abroad (an immigrant, K or V visa), the Phoenix lockbox. The filing locations changed on October 7, 2026.

- **The posture:** the consular refusal (the DOS case number, the post, and the refusal letter citing the grounds), or the I-485 receipt, or the TPS I-821 receipt.
- **Every ground, disclosed in full:** an approved waiver covers only the grounds and conduct the application discloses, so ask about all of them. Criminal grounds: certified court dispositions for every arrest and conviction (the instructions say an uncertified copy isn't enough) and police clearances from each country lived in. Fraud or misrepresentation: the application or interview where it happened and the consular finding. Health: the medical report (for Class A tuberculosis, the Part 11 statement with the local health department). Unlawful presence: entry and departure records.
- **Grounds this form can't waive** are a `risk` item and an `attorney_decision`: a false claim to U.S. citizenship made on or after September 30, 1996; a conviction for murder or torture (212(h)); reentry after unlawful presence or removal under 212(a)(9)(C), except for VAWA, NACARA and HRIFA applicants (otherwise an I-212 after ten years abroad); and, for someone previously admitted as a permanent resident, the 212(h) bars the authorities file lists.
- **The qualifying relative depends on the ground** (instructions, "Reasons for Inadmissibility"): unlawful presence and fraud, a U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse or parent (or the K visa petitioner); criminal grounds under 212(h)(1)(B), a U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse, parent, son or daughter (or the K visa petitioner). A VAWA self-petitioner may show hardship to themselves for fraud, and an approved one needs no hardship showing for criminal grounds. Ask for the relationship and status evidence for that relative, as `i601a.md` lists it.
- **Criminal grounds without hardship:** 15 years since the conduct, evidence of rehabilitation (steady work, family responsibilities, treatment, community service, no further arrests), and that admission isn't against the national welfare or safety.
- **A violent or dangerous crime** raises the standard to exceptional and extremely unusual hardship (8 C.F.R. 212.7(d)): a `risk` item for the attorney.
- **Extreme hardship:** the same evidence as for the I-601A (`i601a.md`), with the statement in Part 5 item 9 or an attached letter (item 9 then says the letter is attached).
- **Discretion:** the statement in Part 6 item 9 (or an attached letter) and the favorable-factor evidence `i601a.md` lists.
- **Previously removed:** an I-212 is also needed and may be filed with the I-601 (Part 1 item 19).
- **Fee:** $1,050; none for a Special Immigrant Juvenile, a T or U applicant, a VAWA self-petitioner, an abused spouse or child under the Cuban Adjustment Act or HRIFA, NACARA, or an Afghan or Iraqi special immigrant (run `fees.py`). Biometrics, when USCIS requires them, come after filing.
