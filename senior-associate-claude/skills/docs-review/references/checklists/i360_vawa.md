# VAWA Self-Petition Or VAWA-Based Adjustment

`matter_type`: I-360 VAWA self-petition packet, or I-360/I-485 VAWA self-petition and adjustment packet (`i360_vawa`).

Sources (checked 2026-10-28): 8 C.F.R. 204.2(c); I-360 instructions (edition 01/20/25), pp. 5-6; I-485 instructions (edition 09/18/26), "VAWA Self-Petitioner"; 3 USCIS-PM D. USCIS considers any credible evidence (INA 204(a)(1)(J)), so a missing conventional record is `partial` with an explanation to request, not a dead end. The matter is protected (8 U.S.C. 1367; the senior-associate skill's `references/protected-matters.md`).

Elements and the evidence for each (spouse; child; or parent of a U.S. citizen son or daughter 21 or older):

- **Qualifying relationship and the abuser's status.** The abuser's U.S. birth certificate, passport, naturalization certificate, or green card, or, when the client has none, the abuser's name, date of birth, and any A-number so USCIS can check its own records (8 C.F.R. 204.1(g)(3)). Marriage certificate and every prior divorce of both spouses, or the birth certificate showing the parent-child link. A self-petition may still be filed within two years of a divorce connected to the abuse, the U.S. citizen abuser's death, or the abuser's loss of status tied to domestic violence.
- **Residence with the abuser,** now or in the past: a lease, mortgage, utility bills, school, medical, employment, or insurance records showing the shared address, or affidavits.
- **Battery or extreme cruelty** during the marriage or while living with the parent: police reports, protective orders and court records, medical and counseling records, shelter records, reports or letters from clergy, social workers, or school officials, photos of injuries with affidavits, and messages. Acts that don't look violent on their own (isolation, immigration threats, control of money or papers, threats about the children) can be extreme cruelty as part of an overall pattern (8 C.F.R. 204.2(c)(1)(vi)).
- **Good moral character** (age 14 and over): the client's affidavit, with a local police clearance or state background check from each place in the U.S. or abroad where the client lived six months or more during the three years before filing; an explanation and other evidence where one is unavailable. Check the address history against the clearances, place by place.
- **Good-faith marriage** (spouses): joint lease, accounts, insurance, or taxes, children together, photos over time, and affidavits about the courtship, wedding, and shared life. Affidavits matter more here than in an I-130, because the abuser often controlled the joint records.
- **Derivative children:** a birth certificate for each unmarried child under 21 listed on the self-petition (8 C.F.R. 204.2(c)(4)); a child who turns 21 before becoming a resident is treated as a self-petitioner with the same priority date (INA 204(a)(1)(D)). In an adjustment, each child files a separate I-485 as a derivative; there are none when the principal is a self-petitioning parent.

If adjustment is in scope: identity documents, the I-797 for an I-360 already filed, the I-693 ("The medical exam" in the common rules in `../evidence-checklists.md`), photos, and anything bearing on inadmissibility. No proof of inspection and admission is needed, and no I-864; the client requests the exemption in I-485 Part 3. No filing fee for the I-360, the I-485 or the (c)(9) I-765 (G-1055, ed. 10/26/26, checked 2026-10-29).

Records that show where the client lives now (a current lease, a child's school record, a photo with location data) need care. Note each one in the memo, ask the attorney before it becomes an exhibit when it isn't needed for an element, and never repeat the current address or location in the client request or anything else a third party might read.
