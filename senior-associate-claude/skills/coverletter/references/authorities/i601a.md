## Propositions

**A1. Unlawful presence of more than 180 days but less than a year, followed by departure, bars admission for 3 years; a year or more bars admission for 10 years.**
- INA § 212(a)(9)(B)(i)(I)–(II), 8 U.S.C. § 1182(a)(9)(B)(i): (II) covers one who "has been unlawfully present in the United States for one year or more, and who again seeks admission within 10 years". Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A2. USCIS may waive the bar for the spouse, son or daughter of a U.S. citizen or LPR on extreme hardship to the citizen or LPR spouse or parent; the decision is discretionary and unreviewable.**
- INA § 212(a)(9)(B)(v): "would result in extreme hardship to the citizen or lawfully resident spouse or parent of such alien"; "No court shall have jurisdiction to review". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(2)(i): "the decision whether to approve a provisional unlawful presence waiver application is discretionary." Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A3. Eligibility: present in the U.S. at filing, biometrics, inadmissible only under 212(a)(9)(B)(i) on departure, a case pending with DOS on an approved immigrant visa petition with the IV fee paid (or DV selection), and departure for the visa interview.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(3)(i)–(vi); (iv)(A): "An approved immigrant visa petition, for which the Department of State immigrant visa processing fee has been paid". Derivative beneficiaries are recognized in the § 212.7(e)(5) rejection list ("Eligibility as a derivative beneficiary of an approved immigrant visa petition"). Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A4. Ineligible: under 17; no pending DOS case; in removal proceedings not administratively closed; a final order without a prior I-212 grant; a reinstated order; or a pending application for LPR status.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(4)(i)–(vi); (iii): "unless the removal proceedings are administratively closed and have not been recalendared at the time of filing". Current, checked 2026-10-30. The current list has no voluntary-departure bar.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(2)(ii): a pending or approved waiver "does not support the filing of any application for interim immigration benefits". Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A5. The applicant proves eligibility, and that discretion should be exercised favorably, by a preponderance of the evidence.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(7): "The alien has the burden to establish, by a preponderance of the evidence, eligibility ... including that the alien merits a favorable exercise of discretion." Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM B.6(B): "The applicant bears the burden of proving that the qualifying relative would suffer extreme hardship." Checked 2026-10-30.
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): the standard "requires that the evidence demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30.

**A6. Only the U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent is a qualifying relative. Hardship to the applicant or to children counts only through its effect on that relative.**
- 9 USCIS-PM B.4(D): non-qualifying relatives' hardship counts "only to the extent that such hardship affects one or more qualifying relatives." Checked 2026-10-30.
- I-601A Instructions (01/20/25): "A U.S. citizen or LPR child is not a qualifying relative for the purpose of showing extreme hardship in this application." Checked 2026-10-30.

**A7. Extreme hardship means hardship beyond the usual or expected. Common consequences of a denial aren't enough on their own, but they count cumulatively, judged on the totality of the circumstances.**
- 9 USCIS-PM B.2(B): "to be considered 'extreme,' the hardship must exceed that which is usual or expected." Checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM B.5(B)–(C): "The common consequences of denying admission, in and of themselves, do not warrant a finding of extreme hardship"; yet they "may result in extreme hardship when assessed cumulatively with other factors." Checked 2026-10-30.
- *Matter of O-J-O-*, 21 I&N Dec. 381, 383 (BIA 1996) (doc 718), quoting *Ige*: "Relevant factors, though not extreme in themselves, must be considered in the aggregate in determining whether extreme hardship exists." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol21/3280.pdf). ImmCite caution: limited by *Monreal-Aguinaga*, 23 I&N Dec. 56 (BIA 2001), only for cancellation hardship; 10 of 10 precedential citing decisions reviewed. Checked 2026-10-30.

**A8. The established factors are U.S. family ties, ties abroad, conditions in the relocation country, financial impact, and health conditions tied to the availability of care. The Policy Manual treats some factors as particularly significant.**
- *Matter of Cervantes-Gonzalez*, 22 I&N Dec. 560, 566 (BIA 1999) (doc 618): factors include "significant conditions of health, particularly when tied to an unavailability of suitable medical care in the country to which the qualifying relative would relocate" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol22/3380.pdf). At 565: extreme hardship "is not a definable term of fixed and inflexible meaning". ImmCite treatment_available; 5 of 5 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; review denied, 232 F.3d 684 (9th Cir. 2000). Checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM B.5(E) lists factors that "often weigh heavily in support of finding extreme hardship": a qualifying relative's prior SIV, T, asylee or refugee status; disability; military service; DOS travel warnings; and substantial displacement of care of the applicant's children. Checked 2026-10-30.
- *Matter of Kao and Lin*, 23 I&N Dec. 45, 50 (BIA 2001) (doc 552) (suspension case): uprooting a teenager "to survive in a Chinese-only environment would be a significant disruption that would constitute extreme hardship" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol23/3446.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 4 of 4 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30. Use it only through the qualifying relative (A6).

**A9. Separation or relocation: the applicant need not prove extreme hardship under both. Extreme hardship under the scenario that would actually result is enough, and a credible sworn statement of the qualifying relative's intent generally shows which one it is.**
- 9 USCIS-PM B.4(B): "an applicant is not required to show extreme hardship under both scenarios"; the applicant may prevail "by showing that either relocation or separation would result in extreme hardship." Checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM B.4(B): "a credible, sworn statement from the qualifying relative of his or her intent to relocate or separate would generally suffice". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A10. Extreme hardship permits but never compels approval: favorable factors must outweigh unfavorable ones.**
- 9 USCIS-PM B.7: "A finding of extreme hardship permits but never compels a favorable exercise of discretion." Checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM A.5(A): approval "depends on whether the favorable factors in the alien's case outweigh the unfavorable ones." Checked 2026-10-30.

**A11. An approved waiver takes effect only after departure, the visa interview and a DOS finding of eligibility, and it waives only the unlawful presence bars. Approval is revoked automatically on a visa denial for another ground, loss of the petition, termination of the registration, or a later unlawful entry.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(12)(i)(A)–(C), (iii); (12)(i): "Does not take effect unless, and until, the alien ... Departs from the United States". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(13): until then "USCIS may reopen and reconsider its decision at any time"; after, the unlawful presence "is waived indefinitely". (e)(14)(i)–(iv) automatic revocation. Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A12. A denial has no appeal or motion, but a new I-601A may be filed, or an I-601 after the consular interview.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(11): "There is no administrative appeal from a denial of a request for a provisional unlawful presence waiver". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(e)(9)(i): "A denial is without prejudice to the alien's filing another provisional unlawful presence waiver application"; an (a)(1) I-601 may follow the interview. (e)(8): USCIS may deny without an RFE or NOID. Current, checked 2026-10-30.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- *Matter of Pilch*, 21 I&N Dec. 627, 633 (BIA 1996) (doc 700): no extreme hardship without "significant hardships over and above the normal economic and social disruptions involved in deportation" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol21/3298.pdf). ImmCite negative: superseded in part by IIRIRA for cancellation (*Monreal-Aguinaga*), but the extreme-hardship holding still stands; 11 of 11 precedential citing decisions reviewed. Checked 2026-10-30. Expect economic loss and separation alone to be called common consequences (9 USCIS-PM B.5(B) lists "Family separation" and "Economic detriment").
- *Cervantes-Gonzalez*, 22 I&N Dec. at 566–67: the spouse "knew that the respondent was in deportation proceedings at the time they were married"; equities acquired with knowledge of the immigration problem carry less weight.
- *Matter of Ige*, 20 I&N Dec. 880, 885 (BIA 1994) (doc 885): if a child would not suffer extreme hardship abroad, hardship from staying behind "would be the result of parental choice, not the parent's deportation" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol20/3230.pdf). The connector infers the page as 886, but the page-885 footer follows the passage; confirm against the PDF. ImmCite caution: limited by *Calderon-Hernandez*, 25 I&N Dec. 885 (BIA 2012), and the parental-choice rule was rejected in the Ninth Circuit (*Perez v. INS*, 96 F.3d 390); 16 of 17 precedential citing decisions reviewed. Checked 2026-10-30. 9 USCIS-PM B.4(B) now accepts a sworn statement of intent, so answer *Ige* with that statement.
- 9 USCIS-PM A.5 carries a 2026-06-05 alert: a D.R.I. order in *Dorcas International Institute of Rhode Island v. USCIS* vacated PM 602-0192, PM 602-0194 and PA 2025-26, with updated instructions pending. Re-read A.5 before arguing discretion.
- USCIS may deny without an RFE (A12), so file a complete hardship record the first time.
