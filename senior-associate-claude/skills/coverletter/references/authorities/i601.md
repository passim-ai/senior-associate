## Propositions

**A1. An applicant for an immigrant visa, adjustment of status, or a K or V visa who is inadmissible on a waivable ground applies on the USCIS form. An approved waiver covers only the grounds and events specified in it, and is generally valid indefinitely.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(a)(1): "an applicant for an immigrant visa, adjustment of status, or a K or V nonimmigrant visa who is inadmissible ... may apply for the related waiver". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(a)(4)(i): approval "applies only to the grounds of inadmissibility, and the related crimes, events, or incidents that are specified in the application"; (a)(4)(ii) valid indefinitely, except that K-1/K-2 waivers are conditioned on the marriage ((a)(4)(iii)) and CPR waivers on removal of conditions ((a)(4)(iv)). Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A2. Unlawful presence (212(a)(9)(B)(v)): extreme hardship to the U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent, or, under the instructions, to the K visa petitioner.**
- INA § 212(a)(9)(B)(v): "would result in extreme hardship to the citizen or lawfully resident spouse or parent of such alien"; decisions are unreviewable. Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- I-601 Instructions (01/20/25): "your qualifying U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident relative (spouse or parent) or K visa petitioner would experience extreme hardship". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A3. Fraud or misrepresentation (212(i)): extreme hardship to the U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or parent (or a K-1/K-2 applicant's U.S. citizen fiancé(e) petitioner). VAWA self-petitioners may also rely on hardship to themselves or to a U.S. citizen, LPR or qualified-alien parent or child. A false claim to citizenship made on or after 9/30/1996 can't be waived.**
- INA § 212(i)(1): waives "clause (i) of subsection (a)(6)(C)"; VAWA: "extreme hardship to the alien or the alien's United States citizen, lawful permanent resident, or qualified alien parent or child". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(1): "U.S. citizen or LPR children are not qualifying relatives"; also "U.S. citizen fiancé(e) petitioner (for K-1 or K-2 visa applicants only)". Checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM F.1(B): a false claim made on or after September 30, 1996 "cannot be waived through a waiver for fraud or willful misrepresentation." Checked 2026-10-30.

**A4. Criminal (212(h)): covers 212(a)(2)(A)(i)(I), (B), (D) and (E), and a single simple-possession offense involving 30 grams or less of marijuana. Three routes: (h)(1)(A) conduct more than 15 years old (or prostitution only) plus rehabilitation and no threat to national welfare, safety or security; (h)(1)(B) extreme hardship to a U.S. citizen or LPR spouse, parent, son or daughter; or (h)(1)(C) VAWA.**
- INA § 212(h)(1)(A)(i)–(iii): "inadmissible only under subparagraph (D)(i) or (D)(ii) ... or the activities ... occurred more than 15 years before the date of the alien's application"; "(iii) the alien has been rehabilitated". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- INA § 212(h)(1)(B): "extreme hardship to the United States citizen or lawfully resident spouse, parent, son, or daughter of such alien"; (h)(1)(C): "the alien is a VAWA self-petitioner". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- *Matter of Shaughnessy*, 12 I&N Dec. 810, 814 (BIA 1968) (doc 2302): "the statute makes no provision for 'extreme hardship' to the alien" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol12/1887.pdf). The connector infers the page; confirm the pin. ImmCite treatment_available; 7 of 7 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30.

**A5. 212(h) bars: murder or torture (including attempt or conspiracy); and, for someone previously admitted as an LPR, an aggravated felony since admission or less than 7 years' continuous lawful residence before removal proceedings began.**
- INA § 212(h) (flush text): "No waiver shall be provided under this subsection in the case of an alien who has been convicted of (or who has admitted committing acts that constitute) murder or criminal acts involving torture"; LPR bar: "has not lawfully resided continuously in the United States for a period of not less than 7 years". Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A6. Violent or dangerous crimes: discretion is generally withheld unless there are extraordinary circumstances or exceptional and extremely unusual hardship, and even that may not be enough.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(d): "except in extraordinary circumstances ... or cases in which an alien clearly demonstrates that the denial ... would result in exceptional and extremely unusual hardship." Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- *Matter of Jean*, 23 I&N Dec. 373, 383 (A.G. 2002) (doc 526): "depending on the gravity of the alien's underlying criminal offense, such a showing might still be insufficient" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol23/3472.pdf). This is the source of § 212.7(d); it arose under INA 209(c). ImmCite treatment_available; 31 of 32 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; one unreviewed (*Rivas-Gomez v. Gonzales*, 441 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 2006)); review denied, 452 F.3d 392 (5th Cir. 2006). Checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM A.5(C) applies the same test. Checked 2026-10-30.

**A7. Every waiver is discretionary. The applicant must show that favorable factors outweigh unfavorable ones, and extreme hardship permits but never compels approval.**
- *Matter of Mendez-Moralez*, 21 I&N Dec. 296, 300 (BIA 1996) (doc 726): the adjudicator "must balance the adverse factors evidencing an alien's undesirability as a permanent resident with the social and humane considerations presented on his behalf" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol21/3272.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 37 of 37 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM A.5(A): approval "depends on whether the favorable factors in the alien's case outweigh the unfavorable ones"; 9 USCIS-PM B.7: "A finding of extreme hardship permits but never compels a favorable exercise of discretion." Checked 2026-10-30.

**A8. Other waivers, briefly.**
- INA § 212(g)(1): communicable disease, for the spouse, unmarried son or daughter, or minor adopted child of a U.S. citizen, LPR or immigrant visa holder, for the parent of such a son or daughter, or for a VAWA self-petitioner; (g)(2) vaccination; (g)(3) physical or mental disorder, on terms set by regulation. Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- INA § 212(d)(11): smuggling, where the alien aided "only an individual who at the time of such action was the alien's spouse, parent, son, or daughter"; for humanitarian purposes, family unity, or the public interest. Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- INA § 212(d)(12): document fraud (212(a)(6)(F)), with no prior civil penalty, "committed solely to assist, aid, or support the alien's spouse or child". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- INA § 212(a)(3)(D)(iv): totalitarian party membership, for close family of U.S. citizens and LPRs, "if the immigrant is not a threat to the security of the United States." Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A9. Extreme hardship is hardship beyond the usual, judged cumulatively on the totality of the circumstances. Hardship to non-qualifying relatives, including the applicant, counts only through the qualifying relative, except for VAWA self-petitioners.**
- 9 USCIS-PM B.2(B): "the hardship must exceed that which is usual or expected"; B.5(B)–(C): common consequences "in and of themselves, do not warrant" a finding but "may result in extreme hardship when assessed cumulatively with other factors"; B.4(D): non-qualifying hardship counts "only to the extent that such hardship affects one or more qualifying relatives." Checked 2026-10-30.
- *Matter of O-J-O-*, 21 I&N Dec. 381, 383 (BIA 1996) (doc 718), quoting *Ige*: "Relevant factors, though not extreme in themselves, must be considered in the aggregate in determining whether extreme hardship exists." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol21/3280.pdf). ImmCite caution: limited by *Monreal-Aguinaga*, 23 I&N Dec. 56 (BIA 2001), only for cancellation hardship; 10 of 10 precedential citing decisions reviewed. Checked 2026-10-30.

**A10. The established factors for 212(i) and similar waivers, plus the Policy Manual's particularly significant factors.**
- *Matter of Cervantes-Gonzalez*, 22 I&N Dec. 560, 566 (BIA 1999) (doc 618) (212(i)): factors include "the conditions in the country or countries to which the qualifying relative would relocate and the extent of the qualifying relative's ties to such countries" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol22/3380.pdf). At 565, the Board found the factors from suspension and other waiver cases "to be helpful". ImmCite treatment_available; 5 of 5 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; review denied, 232 F.3d 684 (9th Cir. 2000). Checked 2026-10-30.
- 9 USCIS-PM B.5(E): factors that "often weigh heavily in support of finding extreme hardship" (prior SIV, T, asylee or refugee status; disability; military service; DOS travel warnings; displacement of care of the applicant's children). Checked 2026-10-30.
- *Matter of Kao and Lin*, 23 I&N Dec. 45, 50 (BIA 2001) (doc 552) (suspension case): forcing a teenager "to survive in a Chinese-only environment would be a significant disruption that would constitute extreme hardship" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol23/3446.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 4 of 4 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30. Most useful in 212(h), where a son or daughter is a qualifying relative.

**A11. Separation or relocation: proving either scenario is enough, and a credible sworn statement of intent generally establishes which will occur.**
- 9 USCIS-PM B.4(B): "an applicant is not required to show extreme hardship under both scenarios"; "a credible, sworn statement from the qualifying relative of his or her intent to relocate or separate would generally suffice". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A12. The applicant proves eligibility and discretion by a preponderance of the evidence.**
- 9 USCIS-PM B.6(B): "The applicant bears the burden of proving that the qualifying relative would suffer extreme hardship"; 9 USCIS-PM A.5: the applicant "bears the burden of proving that he or she merits a favorable exercise of discretion." Checked 2026-10-30.
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): the evidence must "demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- *Matter of Pilch*, 21 I&N Dec. 627, 633 (BIA 1996) (doc 700): no extreme hardship without "significant hardships over and above the normal economic and social disruptions involved in deportation" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol21/3298.pdf). ImmCite negative: superseded in part by IIRIRA for cancellation, but the extreme-hardship holding still stands; 11 of 11 precedential citing decisions reviewed. Checked 2026-10-30. 9 USCIS-PM B.5(B) lists "Family separation" and "Economic detriment" as common consequences.
- *Cervantes-Gonzalez*, 22 I&N Dec. at 566–67: a spouse who "knew that the respondent was in deportation proceedings at the time they were married" gets less weight for after-acquired equities.
- *Matter of Ige*, 20 I&N Dec. 880, 885 (BIA 1994) (doc 885): hardship to a child who stays behind is "the result of parental choice, not the parent's deportation" where relocation would cause no hardship (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol20/3230.pdf). The connector infers page 886; confirm against the PDF. ImmCite caution: limited by *Calderon-Hernandez*, 25 I&N Dec. 885 (BIA 2012), and rejected in the Ninth Circuit; 16 of 17 precedential citing decisions reviewed. Checked 2026-10-30. Answer it with the 9 USCIS-PM B.4(B) sworn statement.
- 9 USCIS-PM B.7: extreme hardship "may not be sufficient to warrant a favorable exercise of discretion with respect to crime- or fraud-related grounds"; 9 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(3): the fraud itself "is the first negative factor to consider." Address the underlying conduct directly.
- 9 USCIS-PM A.5 carries a 2026-06-05 alert: a D.R.I. order in *Dorcas International Institute of Rhode Island v. USCIS* vacated PM 602-0192, PM 602-0194 and PA 2025-26, with updated instructions pending. Re-read A.5 before arguing discretion.
- No 212(i) waiver for false claims to citizenship on or after 9/30/1996 (A3), and no 212(h) waiver past the murder, torture and LPR bars (A5).

## Targeted correction checked November 9 2026

*Matter of A-V-O-*, 30 I&N Dec. 44, 54 (BIA Sept. 17, 2026), overruled *Matter of Kanga* and applies INA 212(a)(8)(A) to aggravated-felony convictions within its prospective scope. The rule applies to immigrants whose aggravated-felony conviction is based on a guilty or nolo contendere plea made, or jury verdict entered, **after September 17, 2026**. Do not substitute sentencing date, apply it retroactively, or assume every criminal conviction is an aggravated felony. Section 212(h) does not waive 212(a)(8)(A). An affected adjustment requires attorney analysis of the criminal record, categorical issues, jurisdiction, and any subsequent treatment before a filing theory is selected. Primary decision: https://www.justice.gov/eoir/media/1461651/dl. This targeted correction does not re-verify the remaining entries.
