## Propositions

**A1. The conditional resident and the petitioning spouse file jointly during the 90 days before the second anniversary of conditional residence.**
- INA § 216(d)(2)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1186a(d)(2)(A): "must be filed during the 90-day period before the second anniversary of the alien's obtaining the status of lawful admission for permanent residence" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1186a/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.4(a)(1) (same window; filing requires the fee, the (a)(5) evidence, and both signatures) (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-216.4). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. The petition shows the marriage was valid where celebrated, has not ended, and was not entered to obtain immigration status, and that no fee or other consideration, other than an attorney's fee for preparing a lawful petition, was given for filing the original immigrant petition.**
- INA § 216(d)(1)(A)(i)(I)–(III), 8 U.S.C. § 1186a(d)(1)(A)(i): (III) "was not entered into for the purpose of procuring an alien's admission as an immigrant" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1186a/). Current, checked 2026-10-29.
- INA § 216(d)(1)(A)(ii), 8 U.S.C. § 1186a(d)(1)(A)(ii): "no fee or other consideration was given (other than a fee or other consideration to an attorney for assistance in preparation of a lawful petition)" for filing the petition under section 1154(a) (same URL; current through Pub. L. 119-136). Checked 2026-10-29. The element is about payment for the petition, not about filing fees.
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.4(c)(1)–(4) lists the same four determinations. Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 6 USCIS-PM I.3(A): "Generally, in order to remove conditions, the CPR must establish that a qualifying marriage is or was bona fide." (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-i-chapter-3). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. If those facts are true, USCIS removes the conditions as of the second anniversary.**
- INA § 216(c)(3)(B), 8 U.S.C. § 1186a(c)(3)(B): "determines that such facts and information are true, the Secretary ... shall so notify the parties involved and shall remove the conditional basis" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1186a/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. The petition is filed with evidence of a good-faith marriage: joint property, a joint lease, commingled finances, children's birth certificates, third-party affidavits, or other proof.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.4(a)(5)(i)–(vi): "Form I-751 shall be accompanied by evidence that the marriage was not entered into for the purpose of evading the immigration laws" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-216.4). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. Good faith turns on the couple's intent when they married, and their conduct afterward is evidence of that intent.**
- *Matter of Laureano*, 19 I&N Dec. 1, 2–3 (BIA 1983) (doc 898): "The central question is whether the bride and groom intended to establish a life together at the time they were married." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/2951.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 28 of 28 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; followed by *Matter of Bador*, 28 I&N Dec. 638 (BIA 2022). Checked 2026-10-28.
- *Laureano*, 19 I&N Dec. at 3: "The conduct of the parties after marriage is relevant to their intent at the time of marriage." Same status.

**A6. Separation or a pending divorce does not by itself defeat a joint petition.**
- 6 USCIS-PM I.4(A): "USCIS may not deny a joint petition solely because the spouses are separated or have initiated divorce or annulment proceedings." (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-i-chapter-4). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.
- *Matter of McKee*, 17 I&N Dec. 332, 334 (BIA 1980) (visa petition): "it was error for the District Director to deny the instant visa petition based solely on the separation of the parties"; at 333, a sham marriage "is intrinsically different from a nonviable or nonsubsisting one" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol17/2782.pdf). Read at the source 2026-10-29; the EOIR precedent list shows no later history. Not yet run through ImmCite: check its treatment with the connector before relying on it.

**A7. The petitioners prove eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence.**
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375–76 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): the standard "requires that the evidence demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; followed by *Matter of Texperts, Inc.*, 29 I&N Dec. 491, 493 (AAO 2026) (re-checked at the source 2026-10-29). Checked 2026-10-28.

**A8. USCIS may waive the interview when the record shows a good-faith marriage.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.4(b)(1): "If satisfied that the marriage was not for the purpose of evading the immigration laws, the ... director may waive the interview and approve the petition" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-216.4). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A9. A properly filed I-751 extends conditional resident status until USCIS decides it.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.4(a)(1): "the alien's conditional permanent resident status shall be extended automatically, if necessary, until such time as the director has adjudicated the petition" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-216.4). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A10. A late joint petition may be accepted for good cause and extenuating circumstances, explained in writing.**
- INA § 216(d)(2)(B), 8 U.S.C. § 1186a(d)(2)(B): "only if the alien establishes to the satisfaction of the Secretary of Homeland Security good cause and extenuating circumstances for failure to file" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1186a/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.4(a)(6) (written showing of good cause to the director). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.4(c): if derogatory information on any of the four issues surfaces, USCIS offers a chance to rebut; if it isn't overcome, USCIS may deny, terminate status, and issue an NTA. Answer a known doubt in the letter with evidence rather than leave it for the interview.
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.4(a)(6): an untimely filing terminates status automatically. Any late filing needs its good-cause explanation in the letter itself.

## Not covered here (research per client)
- Divorce or separation during pendency (amending to a waiver, 6 USCIS-PM I.4), a prior 204(c) or fraud finding, criminal history, a spouse overseas on military or government orders (6 USCIS-PM I.3(D)), children filing separately, and any client-specific good-cause facts for a late filing.
