# Standard authorities: I-129F fiancé(e) petition (`i129f`)

Verified 2026-10-28 with the BIA Edge connector. Before citing, run the currency check in `../research.md`; refresh the whole file once it is more than 180 days old.

Case IDs for `check_validity`: 300. Update-feed topic: `visas/family reunification`.

The Policy Manual has no K-1 chapter (searched 2026-10-28), so the form instructions carry the evidence points.

## Propositions

**A1. A K-1 fiancé(e) of a U.S. citizen seeks admission solely to marry the petitioner within 90 days.**
- INA § 101(a)(15)(K)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15)(K)(i): "seeks to enter the United States solely to conclude a valid marriage with the petitioner within ninety days after admission" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1101/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. USCIS approves when the couple met in person within the 2 years before filing, intend in good faith to marry, and are legally able and actually willing to marry within 90 days of arrival.**
- INA § 214(d)(1), 8 U.S.C. § 1184(d)(1): "the parties have previously met in person within 2 years before the date of filing the petition, have a bona fide intention to marry" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1184/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- Same: "are legally able and actually willing to conclude a valid marriage in the United States within a period of ninety days". Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. The in-person meeting requirement may be waived for extreme hardship to the petitioner or where meeting would violate strict, long-established custom.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(k)(2): "the director may exempt the petitioner from this requirement only if it is established that compliance would result in extreme hardship to the petitioner" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-214.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28. The custom exception also requires showing every other part of the traditional arrangement has been or will be met. (The 2026 duration-of-status rule stayed by court order touches paragraphs (f), (i), and (j) of § 214.2, not (k).)
- INA § 214(d)(1): the Secretary "in his discretion may waive the requirement that the parties have previously met in person". Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. Intent to marry is shown by signed statements from both partners or other evidence; the meeting by statements, tickets, passport pages, or similar proof.**
- USCIS, Instructions for Form I-129F (ed. 01/20/25), Part 8.A: "Evidence of your intention to marry may include statements of intent to marry signed by both you and your fiancé(e)" (https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/i-129finstr.pdf). Current edition per connector (record updated 2026-10-26), checked 2026-10-28.
- Same, Part 8.B: "a written statement from you and/or your fiancé(e) stating the circumstances of your meeting, a copy of airline tickets, passport pages". Checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. The petitioner proves eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence.**
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375–76 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): the standard "requires that the evidence demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; followed by *Matter of Texperts, Inc.*, 29 I&N Dec. 491, 493 (AAO 2026) (re-checked at the source 2026-10-29). Checked 2026-10-28.
- I-129F Instructions, Part 8.A, applies the same standard to "your mutual intention". Checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. The petition discloses the petitioner's convictions for specified crimes and related protection orders (IMBRA).**
- INA § 214(d)(1): "Such information shall include information on any criminal convictions of the petitioner for any specified crime" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1184/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A7. A petitioner who previously filed K-1 petitions for two or more fiancé(e)s, or had one approved within the last 2 years, needs a discretionary waiver.**
- INA § 214(d)(2)(A)–(B), 8 U.S.C. § 1184(d)(2): approval requires that "the petitioner has not, previous to the pending petition, petitioned under paragraph (1) with respect to two or more applying aliens" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1184/). Current, checked 2026-10-28. Subparagraph (B) allows a waiver "if justification exists", generally not where the petitioner has a record of violent offenses.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(k)(2): failure to prove the meeting, or a basis to waive it, "shall result in the denial of the petition" (without prejudice to refiling). Put the meeting evidence up front.
- INA § 214(d)(1): if the marriage doesn't take place within three months after admission, the K-1 and children must depart. The letter shouldn't suggest any later wedding date.

## Not covered here (research per client)
- IMBRA waivers where the petitioner has a violent-offense record (INA § 214(d)(2)(B)–(C)), prior marriages and their termination, a 204(c) or fraud history, the beneficiary's inadmissibility at the consulate, and K-3 or post-arrival adjustment questions.
