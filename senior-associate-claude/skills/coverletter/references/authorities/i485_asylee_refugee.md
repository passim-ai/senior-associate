# Standard authorities: I-485 adjustment for asylees and refugees under INA 209 (`i485_asylee_refugee`)

Verified 2026-10-28 with the BIA Edge connector. Before citing, run the currency check in `../research.md`; refresh the whole file once it is more than 180 days old.

Case IDs for `check_validity`: 526. Update-feed topic: `asylum/refugees`.

## Propositions

**A1. An asylee may adjust, in USCIS's discretion, after one year of physical presence following the grant of asylum.**
- INA § 209(b)(2), 8 U.S.C. § 1159(b)(2): "has been physically present in the United States for at least one year after being granted asylum" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1159/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 209.2(a)(1)(i)–(v) restates the same elements (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-209.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 7 USCIS-PM M.2: "An asylee may adjust status to a lawful permanent resident if the asylee meets the following four requirements" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-m-chapter-2). PM last updated 2026-10-27; checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. The asylee still has to be a refugee (or the spouse or child of one), not firmly resettled elsewhere, and admissible apart from the grounds 209(c) excludes.**
- INA § 209(b)(3)–(5): "continues to be a refugee within the meaning of section 1101(a)(42)(A) of this title or a spouse or child of such a refugee" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1159/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. A refugee admitted under INA 207 whose status hasn't been terminated must apply for adjustment after one year of physical presence.**
- INA § 209(a)(1), 8 U.S.C. § 1159(a)(1): "shall, at the end of such year period, return or be returned to the custody of the Department of Homeland Security for inspection and examination" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1159/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 209.1(a)(1): "is required to apply to USCIS one year after entry" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-209.1). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 7 USCIS-PM L.2: "Physically present in the United States as a refugee for at least 1 year" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-l-chapter-2). PM last updated 2026-10-27; checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. The public charge, labor certification, and immigrant-document grounds (212(a)(4), (5), and (7)(A)) don't apply, so no I-864 or labor certification is needed.**
- INA § 209(c), 8 U.S.C. § 1159(c): "shall not be applicable to any alien seeking adjustment of status under this section" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1159/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 209.1(a)(1) and § 209.2(a)(1)(v) apply the same exclusions (URLs above). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. Most other inadmissibility grounds can be waived for humanitarian purposes, family unity, or the public interest. The drug-trafficking (2)(C) ground and the security grounds in (3)(A), (B), (C), and (E) can't be waived.**
- INA § 209(c): "may waive any other provision of such section ... for humanitarian purposes, to assure family unity, or when it is otherwise in the public interest" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1159/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. An approved asylee's permanent residence is backdated one year from the approval.**
- INA § 209(b) (flush text): "shall establish a record of the alien's admission for lawful permanent residence as of the date one year before the date of the approval" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1159/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- *Matter of Jean*, 23 I&N Dec. 373, 383 (A.G. 2002) (doc 526). A 209(c) waiver will not go to "violent or dangerous individuals except in extraordinary circumstances," and even exceptional and extremely unusual hardship may not be enough (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol23/3472.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 31 of 32 published citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; one unreviewed (*Rivas-Gomez v. Gonzales*, 441 F.3d 1072 (9th Cir. 2006)). Petition for review denied, *Jean v. Gonzales*, 452 F.3d 392 (5th Cir. 2006). Checked 2026-10-28. Raise it only when the record has a crime that needs a waiver.
- 7 USCIS-PM M.2(C): "A principal asylee who has naturalized no longer meets the definition of a refugee." Once the principal naturalizes, or the principal's asylum is terminated, a derivative can't adjust as a derivative.
- *Removing 30-Day Refugee Termination Notice*, 91 Fed. Reg. 65077 (interim final rule, effective 2026-10-14), lets DHS terminate refugee status faster when it finds the person was not a refugee. Refugee adjustment requires unterminated status, so confirm status before filing.
- 8 C.F.R. § 209.2(a)(1)(vi) still mentions an available refugee number under 207(a). INA § 209(b) as codified has no numerical limit, so cite the statute and leave clause (vi) out.

## Not covered here (research per client)
- Firm resettlement facts, persecutor or terrorism-related bars, criminal history and the I-602 waiver equities, trips back to the country of claimed persecution (re-availment and cessation questions), proof of the derivative relationship, nunc pro tunc asylum, and pending termination proceedings.
