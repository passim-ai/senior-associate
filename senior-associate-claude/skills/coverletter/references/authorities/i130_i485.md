# Standard authorities: I-130 / I-485 immediate relative (`i130_i485`)

Case IDs for `check_validity`: 4376, 300, 898, 1062. Update-feed topic: `adjustment of status`.

## Propositions

**A1. A U.S. citizen's spouse, child, or parent (if the citizen is 21 or older) is an immediate relative, outside the numerical limits.**
- INA § 201(b), (b)(2)(A)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1151(b): aliens "who are not subject to the worldwide levels or numerical limitations of subsection (a)"; "the term 'immediate relatives' means the children, spouses, and parents of a citizen of the United States" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1151/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. A U.S. citizen petitions with proof of citizenship and of the relationship; for a spouse, the civil marriage certificate and proof that every prior marriage ended.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1154(a)(1)(A)(i): a citizen claiming "immediate relative status under section 1151(b)(2)(A)(i) ... may file a petition" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1154/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.1(g)(1): "A petition must be accompanied by one of the following" (birth certificate, passport, or naturalization certificate) (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-204.1). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(a)(2): "a certificate of marriage issued by civil authorities, and proof of the legal termination of all previous marriages of both the petitioner and the beneficiary" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-204.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. Parent and child petitions rest on the birth certificate that names the parent.**
- INA § 101(b)(1), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(b)(1): "The term 'child' means an unmarried person under twenty-one years of age" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1101/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(d)(2)(i) (child): "the birth certificate of the child showing the mother's name must accompany the petition"; a petitioning father adds the parents' marriage certificate. § 204.2(f)(2)(i) (parent): "the birth certificate of the petitioner showing the mother's name" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-204.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. The petitioner proves the relationship by a preponderance of the evidence, eligible at filing and through adjudication.**
- *Matter of Brantigan*, 11 I&N Dec. 493, 495 (BIA 1966): "In visa petition proceedings, the burden of proof to establish eligibility sought for the benefit conferred by the immigration laws rests upon the petitioner." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol11/1553.pdf). Read at the source 2026-10-29; not yet run through ImmCite. Background only; *Rehman* states the standard.
- *Matter of Rehman*, 27 I&N Dec. 124, 125 (BIA 2017) (doc 4376): "In visa petition proceedings, the petitioner bears the burden of establishing the claimed relationship by a preponderance of the evidence." (https://www.justice.gov/media/914011/dl?inline). ImmCite treatment_available; both published citing decisions read, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): the standard "requires that the evidence demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(1): "eligible for the requested benefit at the time of filing the benefit request and must continue to be eligible through adjudication" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-103.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. A marriage valid where celebrated is generally valid for immigration purposes.**
- 6 USCIS-PM B.6(B)(1): "Generally, USCIS recognizes a marriage as valid for immigration purposes if it is legally valid in the place where the marriage is celebrated." (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-b-chapter-6). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. Good faith turns on whether the couple intended to build a life together when they married; later conduct is evidence of that intent.**
- 6 USCIS-PM B.6(B)(3): the petitioner must show "the spouses intended to build a life together at the time they were married" (URL as A5). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.
- *Matter of Laureano*, 19 I&N Dec. 1, 2–3 (BIA 1983) (doc 898): "The central question is whether the bride and groom intended to establish a life together at the time they were married." At 3: "The conduct of the parties after marriage is relevant to their intent at the time of marriage." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/2951.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 28 of 28 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.

**A6a. A marriage that has broken down isn't for that reason a sham; separation alone doesn't support a denial.**
- *Matter of McKee*, 17 I&N Dec. 332, 334 (BIA 1980): "it was error for the District Director to deny the instant visa petition based solely on the separation of the parties" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol17/2782.pdf). Read at the source 2026-10-29; the EOIR precedent list shows no later history; not yet run through ImmCite.

**A7. Good-faith evidence includes joint property or lease, commingled finances, children's birth certificates, and third-party affidavits.**
- 6 USCIS-PM B.6(C)(1): "A lease showing joint tenancy of a common residence; ... Documentation of commingling of financial resources; ... Affidavits of third parties with personal knowledge of the relationship" (URL as A5). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A8. Adjustment requires inspection and admission or parole, visa eligibility, admissibility, an available visa, and a favorable exercise of discretion.**
- INA § 245(a), 8 U.S.C. § 1255(a): "inspected and admitted or paroled ... in his discretion ... eligible to receive an immigrant visa and is admissible to the United States for permanent residence" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1255/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A9. An immediate relative may file the I-485 together with the I-130.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 245.2(a)(2)(i)(B): the application "will be considered properly filed whether submitted concurrently with or subsequent to the visa petition" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-245.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A10. The 245(c)(2) and (c)(8) bars (unauthorized work, lapsed status, status violations) don't apply to immediate relatives.**
- INA § 245(c)(2): applies to "an alien (other than an immediate relative as defined in section 1151(b) ...)" (URL as A8). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 7 USCIS-PM B.4: "The INA 245(c)(2) and INA 245(c)(8) bars to adjustment do not apply to: Immediate relatives" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-b-chapter-4). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28. Cite the Policy Manual for the (c)(8) point.

**A11. The applicant submits a civil surgeon's medical examination.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 245.5: the applicant "shall be required to have a medical examination by a designated civil surgeon" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-245.5). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A12. The petitioner's I-864 is required, showing income of at least 125 percent of the poverty line; a sufficient I-864 is one factor in the public charge totality.**
- INA § 212(a)(4)(C)(ii): the petitioner "has executed an affidavit of support described in section 1183a" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1182/). INA § 213A(f)(1)(E): "the means to maintain an annual income equal to at least 125 percent of the Federal poverty line" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1183a/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 USCIS-PM G.6(A): "USCIS only considers it as one factor in the totality of the circumstances" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-8-part-g-chapter-6). PM last updated 2026-10-27 (Part G updated 2026-09-18 to implement new public charge guidance), checked 2026-10-28. Don't say a sufficient I-864 "weighs strongly"; argue the sponsor's specific facts.

**A13. Immediate-relative status is a weighty equity, and family ties are a positive discretionary factor.**
- *Matter of Ibrahim*, 18 I&N Dec. 55, 57 (BIA 1981) (doc 1062): "The Immigration and Nationality Act makes immediate relative status a special and weighty equity." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol18/2866.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none negative. Checked 2026-10-28.
- 7 USCIS-PM A.10(B)(2) (positive factors): "Family ties to the United States and the closeness of the underlying relationships" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-a-chapter-10). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- USCIS, PM-602-0199, *Adjustment of Status is a Matter of Discretion and Administrative Grace* (May 21, 2026), p. 5: "The absence of adverse factors, by itself, does not demonstrate such unusual or outstanding equities." (https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/memos/PM-602-0199-AdjustmentOfStatusAndDiscretion-20260521.pdf; date and quote re-read 2026-10-29). Argue affirmative equities, not a clean record. No rescission found 2026-10-28.
- *Ibrahim*, 18 I&N Dec. at 55 (syllabus 1): preconceived intent to remain "is a serious adverse factor." Relevant after a visitor entry; research per client.
- 7 USCIS-PM A.10 carries a banner about the June 5, 2026 order in *Dorcas Int'l Inst. of R.I. v. USCIS*, No. 26-cv-132-JJM-PAS (D.R.I.), which vacated PM-602-0192, PM-602-0194 and PA-2025-26 (final judgment June 11, 2026; USCIS alert of June 12, 2026, https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/court-order-on-hold-policies). The chapter text under the banner still carries the vacated guidance. Read the banner before relying on the chapter. Checked 2026-10-29; no appeal or stay confirmed at an official source.
- 7 USCIS-PM B (part page, alert of Aug. 13, 2026) implements *Matter of Delcarmen-Lara*, 29 I&N Dec. 830 (BIA 2026): advance-parole travel after more than 180 days of unlawful presence can trigger the 212(a)(9)(B) bar. Affects a companion I-131, not the letter's eligibility argument.

- *Matter of Ho*, 19 I&N Dec. 582, 591–92 (BIA 1988): "it is incumbent upon the petitioner to resolve the inconsistencies by independent objective evidence" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/3051.pdf). An adoption-petition revocation, cited for inconsistencies in any visa petition. Expect it when dates, addresses or names conflict; answer each conflict with a document, not an explanation alone. Read at the source 2026-10-29; not yet run through ImmCite.
- INA 204(c): *Matter of Tawfik*, 20 I&N Dec. 166, 167 (BIA 1990): prior marriage fraud must be "documented in the alien's file and must be substantial and probative" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol20/3130.pdf); *Matter of P. Singh*, 27 I&N Dec. 598, 607 (BIA 2019): the evidence "must establish that it is more than probably true that the marriage is fraudulent" (https://www.justice.gov/media/1022166/dl?inline). Both read at the source 2026-10-29; not yet run through ImmCite. Research per client when the beneficiary has an earlier petition.
- Marriage during proceedings (204(g), 245(e)): *Matter of Arthur*, 20 I&N Dec. 475, 479 (BIA 1992): the presumption of fraud "may, under present law, be overcome only by a showing of evidence that is 'clear and convincing.'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol20/3173.pdf). Its reopening rule was modified by *Matter of Velarde-Pacheco*, 23 I&N Dec. 253, 256 (BIA 2002) (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol23/3463.pdf). Both read at the source 2026-10-29; not yet run through ImmCite. Cite *Arthur* for the standard only, never for reopening.

## Not covered here (research per client)
