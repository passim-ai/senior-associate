## Propositions

**A1. A general applicant needs five years of continuous residence after admission as a permanent resident, physical presence for half that time, three months in the state or district, and good moral character and attachment throughout.**
- INA § 316(a), 8 U.S.C. § 1427(a): "has been physically present therein for periods totaling at least half of that time" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1427/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. The spouse of a U.S. citizen may naturalize after three years of continuous residence while living in marital union with a spouse who was a citizen throughout.**
- INA § 319(a), 8 U.S.C. § 1430(a): "during the three years immediately preceding the date of filing his application has been living in marital union with the citizen spouse" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1430/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 319.1(a)(3): "Has been living in marital union with the citizen spouse for the three years preceding the date of examination" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-319.1). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. The application may be filed up to 90 days before the residence period is complete.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 334.2(b): "An application for naturalization may be filed up to 90 days prior to the completion of the required period of residence" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-334.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. The applicant bears the burden of proving each requirement by a preponderance of the evidence.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 316.2(b): "The applicant shall bear the burden of establishing by a preponderance of the evidence that he or she meets all of the requirements for naturalization" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-316.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. An absence of more than six months but less than one year is presumed to break continuous residence, and the applicant may rebut the presumption.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 316.5(c)(1)(i): "shall disrupt the continuity of such residence ... unless the applicant can establish otherwise to the satisfaction of the Service" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-316.5). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 12 USCIS-PM D.3 (subsection "1. Absence of More than 6 Months (but Less than 1 Year)"): such an absence "is presumed to break the continuity of such residence" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-12-part-d-chapter-3). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. Good moral character is required throughout the statutory period: five years before filing for a general applicant, three for a 319(a) spouse.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 316.10(a)(1): the applicant shows that "during the statutorily prescribed period, he or she has been and continues to be a person of good moral character" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-316.10). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 12 USCIS-PM F.2(A): "the statutory period for GMC for an applicant filing under the general naturalization provision starts 5 years prior to the date of filing" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-12-part-f-chapter-2). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A7. The applicant must show basic English and knowledge of U.S. history and government.**
- INA § 312(a)(1)-(2), 8 U.S.C. § 1423(a): "an understanding of the English language, including an ability to read, write, and speak words in ordinary usage" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1423/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- *Berenyi v. INS*, 385 U.S. 630, 637 (1967) (doc 228378): doubts "should be resolved in favor of the United States and against the claimant." ImmCite treatment_available; 44 of 44 precedential citing decisions reviewed, no negative or overruling treatment. Answer it by proving each element with documents rather than leaving gaps.
- INA § 101(f) (final paragraph), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(f): not falling within a listed bar "shall not preclude a finding that ... such person is or was not of good moral character." A clean record alone doesn't establish good moral character, so present affirmative evidence.
- 8 C.F.R. § 316.10(a)(2): USCIS "is not limited to reviewing the applicant's conduct during the five years immediately preceding the filing." Earlier conduct can count against the applicant.
- 8 C.F.R. § 316.10(b)(3): "Unless the applicant establishes extenuating circumstances, the applicant shall be found to lack good moral character if, during the statutory period" the applicant failed to support dependents, had a marriage-destroying affair, or committed unlawful acts.
- 12 USCIS-PM B.1 (policy alert of Aug. 25, 2026; PM last updated 2026-10-27): "As of November 2025, USCIS is exercising its statutory authority under INA 335 to conduct an investigation ... of all aliens applying for naturalization." Expect residence and character claims to be corroborated, and document them.
