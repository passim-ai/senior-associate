## Propositions

**A1. A U.S. citizen's spouse, child, or parent (if the citizen is 21 or older) is an immediate relative, outside the numerical limits.**
- INA § 201(b), (b)(2)(A)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1151(b): aliens "who are not subject to the worldwide levels or numerical limitations of subsection (a)"; "the term 'immediate relatives' means the children, spouses, and parents of a citizen of the United States" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1151/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. Other relatives immigrate in the numerically limited family preference categories; a permanent resident's spouse or child is F2A.**
- INA § 203(a)(2)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(a)(2)(A): qualified immigrants "who are the spouses or children of an alien lawfully admitted for permanent residence" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1153/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. A citizen petitions for immediate relatives and the F1, F3, and F4 categories; a permanent resident petitions for F2 relatives.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1154(a)(1)(A)(i): a citizen claiming a relationship under "paragraph (1), (3), or (4) of section 1153(a) ... or to an immediate relative status ... may file a petition" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1154/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- INA § 204(a)(1)(B)(i)(I): a permanent resident claiming "the relationship described in section 1153(a)(2) of this title may file a petition" (same URL). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. The petition proves the petitioner's status and the relationship; for a spouse, the civil marriage certificate and proof that every prior marriage ended.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.1(g)(1): "A petition must be accompanied by one of the following" (U.S. birth certificate, qualifying passport, naturalization or citizenship certificate) (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-204.1). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(a)(2): "a certificate of marriage issued by civil authorities, and proof of the legal termination of all previous marriages of both the petitioner and the beneficiary" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-204.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. Parent and child petitions rest on the birth certificate that names the parent.**
- INA § 101(b)(1), 8 U.S.C. § 1101(b)(1): "The term 'child' means an unmarried person under twenty-one years of age" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1101/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(d)(2)(i) (child): "the birth certificate of the child showing the mother's name must accompany the petition"; a petitioning father adds the parents' marriage certificate. § 204.2(f)(2)(i) (parent): "the birth certificate of the petitioner showing the mother's name" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-204.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. The petitioner proves the relationship by a preponderance of the evidence, eligible at filing and through adjudication.**
- *Matter of Rehman*, 27 I&N Dec. 124, 125 (BIA 2017) (doc 4376): "In visa petition proceedings, the petitioner bears the burden of establishing the claimed relationship by a preponderance of the evidence." (https://www.justice.gov/media/914011/dl?inline). ImmCite treatment_available; both published citing decisions read, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): the standard "requires that the evidence demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 103.2(b)(1): "eligible for the requested benefit at the time of filing the benefit request and must continue to be eligible through adjudication" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-103.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A7. A marriage valid where celebrated is generally valid for immigration purposes.**
- 6 USCIS-PM B.6(B)(1): "Generally, USCIS recognizes a marriage as valid for immigration purposes if it is legally valid in the place where the marriage is celebrated." (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-b-chapter-6). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A8. Good faith turns on whether the couple intended to build a life together when they married; later conduct is evidence of that intent.**
- 6 USCIS-PM B.6(B)(3): the petitioner must show "the spouses intended to build a life together at the time they were married" (URL as A7). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.
- *Matter of Laureano*, 19 I&N Dec. 1, 2–3 (BIA 1983) (doc 898): "The central question is whether the bride and groom intended to establish a life together at the time they were married." At 3: "The conduct of the parties after marriage is relevant to their intent at the time of marriage." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/2951.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 28 of 28 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.

**A8a. Separation alone doesn't support denying a spousal petition.**
- *Matter of McKee*, 17 I&N Dec. 332, 334 (BIA 1980): "it was error for the District Director to deny the instant visa petition based solely on the separation of the parties" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol17/2782.pdf). Read at the source 2026-10-29; not yet run through ImmCite.

**A9. Good-faith evidence includes joint property or lease, commingled finances, children's birth certificates, and third-party affidavits.**
- 6 USCIS-PM B.6(C)(1): "A lease showing joint tenancy of a common residence; ... Documentation of commingling of financial resources; ... Affidavits of third parties with personal knowledge of the relationship" (URL as A7). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- *Matter of Tawfik*, 20 I&N Dec. 166, 167 (BIA 1990) (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol20/3130.pdf) and *Matter of P. Singh*, 27 I&N Dec. 598, 607 (BIA 2019) (https://www.justice.gov/media/1022166/dl?inline) set the 204(c) standard the Policy Manual paraphrases: "more than probably true that the marriage is fraudulent." *Matter of Ho*, 19 I&N Dec. 582, 591–92 (BIA 1988): inconsistencies must be resolved "by independent objective evidence" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/3051.pdf). All read at the source 2026-10-29; not yet run through ImmCite.
- 6 USCIS-PM B.6 (section "INA 204(c) Denials"): a prior-fraud denial needs "substantial and probative evidence," which the BIA defines as "more than a preponderance of the evidence, but less than clear and convincing evidence." If the beneficiary has any earlier petition history, research it before filing.
