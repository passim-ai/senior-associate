# Standard authorities: I-751 waiver (`i751_waiver`)

## Propositions

**A1. A conditional resident who can't file jointly may ask USCIS, in its discretion, to waive the joint filing requirement for extreme hardship, a good-faith marriage that has ended, or battery or extreme cruelty.**
- INA § 216(c)(4)(A)–(D), 8 U.S.C. § 1186a(c)(4): (B) applies where "the qualifying marriage was entered into in good faith by the alien spouse, but the qualifying marriage has been terminated" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1186a/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. A waiver can be filed at any time after conditional residence is granted; the 90-day window doesn't apply.**
- 6 USCIS-PM I.5 (introduction): "CPRs may file waiver requests at any time before, during, or after the 90-day period immediately preceding the second anniversary" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-i-chapter-5). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. The good-faith (divorce) waiver needs a final divorce or annulment; separation isn't enough, though a divorce that becomes final during an RFE response period still counts.**
- 6 USCIS-PM I.5(B)(2): "A CPR is only eligible for a good faith (divorce) waiver if the parties have already terminated the qualifying marriage." PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.
- 6 USCIS-PM I.5(B)(2): "The legal termination of the qualifying marriage may occur during the response period to the RFE" (same chapter). Checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. The applicant need not show they weren't at fault for the divorce.**
- 6 USCIS-PM I.5(B)(1): "The CPR does not need to establish that the CPR is not at fault for the terminated marriage." Checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. Good faith is shown by both spouses' commitment to the marriage: combined finances, how long they lived together, children, and other evidence.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.5(e)(2)(i)–(iv): "the director shall consider evidence relating to the amount of commitment by both parties to the marital relationship" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-216.5). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. Good faith turns on intent at the time of the marriage; later conduct, including the breakdown, is evidence of that intent and not the test.**
- *Matter of Laureano*, 19 I&N Dec. 1, 2–3 (BIA 1983) (doc 898): "The central question is whether the bride and groom intended to establish a life together at the time they were married." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/2951.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 28 of 28 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.
- *Laureano*, 19 I&N Dec. at 3: "The conduct of the parties after marriage is relevant to their intent at the time of marriage." Same status.

**A7. Extreme hardship counts only circumstances arising during the conditional period, must exceed the hardship any removal brings, and is the applicant's burden.**
- INA § 216(c)(4) (flush text): "shall consider circumstances occurring only during the period that the alien was admitted for permanent residence on a conditional basis" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1186a/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.5(e)(1): "only in those cases where the hardship is extreme should the application for a waiver be granted" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-216.5). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- *Matter of Munroe*, 26 I&N Dec. 428, 430 (BIA 2014) (doc 184): "the relevant period for determining such hardship is the 2-year period that an alien was admitted for permanent residence on a conditional basis" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol26/3817.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 6 of 6 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28. Limiting: hardship arising only after the 2-year period doesn't count.

**A8. Battery or extreme cruelty includes any act or threat of violence causing or threatening physical or mental injury, and psychological or sexual abuse.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 216.5(e)(3)(i): "being the victim of any act or threatened act of violence ... which results or threatens to result in physical or mental injury" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-216.5). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A9. USCIS considers any credible evidence on a waiver application.**
- INA § 216(c)(4) (flush text): "shall consider any credible evidence relevant to the application" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1186a/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A10. The applicant proves eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence.**
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375–76 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): the standard "requires that the evidence demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- *Matter of Bador*, 28 I&N Dec. 638, 646 (BIA 2022) (doc 93): "A fraudulent marriage cannot be entered into in good faith" (https://www.justice.gov/d9/2024-02/4052_0.pdf); no good-faith waiver after admitting a fraudulent marriage. ImmCite treatment_available; 3 of 3 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; petition for review denied, *Bador v. Garland*, 107 F.4th 75 (2d Cir. 2024). Checked 2026-10-28.
- *Munroe* (A7) narrows hardship to the 2-year conditional period; 8 C.F.R. § 216.5(e)(1) puts the burden "solely with the applicant." Don't argue hardship that arose later.
- INA § 216(c)(4) makes every waiver discretionary, and the Secretary decides what evidence is credible and its weight. Argue the equities, not just eligibility.
