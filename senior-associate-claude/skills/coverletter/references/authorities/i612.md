# Standard authorities: I-612 waiver (`i612`)

Verified 2026-10-28 with the BIA Edge connector. Before citing, run the currency check in `../research.md`; refresh the whole file once it is more than 180 days old.

Case IDs for `check_validity`: 2403, 941. Update-feed topic: `waivers of inadmissibility`.

The connector has no USCIS Policy Manual chapter on 212(e) waivers, but the Policy Manual has one: 2 USCIS-PM D.4, *Waiver of the Foreign Residence Requirement* (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-d-chapter-4, current as of 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-29). Read it at the source when citing.

## Propositions

**A1. A J-1 exchange visitor whose program was government-funded, whose skill is on the home country's skills list, or who came for graduate medical training can't immigrate, adjust, or take H or L status until spending two years abroad. J-2 dependents share the requirement.**
- INA § 212(e), 8 U.S.C. § 1182(e): "has resided and been physically present in the country of his nationality or his last residence for an aggregate of at least two years" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1182/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(c)(1)–(4); (c)(4) on J-2 dependents: "is also subject to that requirement" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-212.7). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. The I-612 waiver rests on exceptional hardship to a U.S. citizen or LPR spouse or child.**
- INA § 212(e) (proviso): "departure from the United States would impose exceptional hardship upon the alien's spouse or child" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1182/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(c)(5): "would impose exceptional hardship upon his/her spouse or child who is a citizen of the United States or a lawful permanent resident alien" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-212.7). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. The alternative ground is persecution on account of race, religion, or political opinion, supported by the applicant's own detailed statement.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(c)(8): "setting forth in detail why the applicant believes he or she would be subject to persecution" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-212.7). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. A hardship application needs the marriage and status documents and a signed, detailed statement that covers the family's income and savings, plus a physician's certificate when the hardship is medical.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(c)(6)–(7): "The statement shall include all pertinent information concerning the incomes and savings of the applicant and spouse." (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-212.7). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(c)(7) (medical grounds): "a medical certificate from a qualified physician setting forth in terms understandable to a layman the nature and effect of the illness" (same URL). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- USCIS, Instructions for Form I-612 (ed. 04/01/24): "in addition to your own required statement detailing the hardship or persecution that will occur if you return" (https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/i-612instr.pdf). Current edition, checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. Exceptional hardship has to be shown both if the spouse goes abroad with the applicant and if the spouse stays in the United States. Ordinary separation isn't enough.**
- *Matter of Mansour*, 11 I&N Dec. 306, 307 (D.D. 1965) (doc 2403): "it must also be shown that the spouse would suffer as the result of having to remain in the United States" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol11/1505.pdf). ImmCite unchecked: no published decision cites it, which is not evidence either way. Checked 2026-10-28.
- *Mansour*, 11 I&N Dec. at 307: "Temporary separation, even though abnormal, is a problem many families face in life and, in and of itself, does not represent exceptional hardship" (same URL). Checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. Approval also needs a favorable State Department recommendation and a finding that admission is in the public interest. USCIS's hardship finding alone doesn't suffice.**
- *Matter of Tayabji*, 19 I&N Dec. 264, 266 (BIA 1985) (doc 941): "the favorable recommendation of the Secretary of State is a necessary prerequisite to the grant of an exceptional hardship waiver under section 212(e)" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/2994.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; all 3 published citing decisions read, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.
- INA § 212(e): "whose admission to the United States is found by the Attorney General to be in the public interest" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1182/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.7(c)(11): "no appeal shall lie from the denial of an application for lack of a favorable recommendation from the Secretary of State" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-212.7). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A7. Officers weigh hardship in both scenarios: if the spouse or child relocates with the applicant, and if they are separated from the applicant.**
- 2 USCIS-PM D.4(B): officers "must evaluate whether" the qualifying relative "would experience exceptional hardship if they were to relocate with the applicant" and "if they were to be separated from the applicant" (URL above). Checked 2026-10-29.
- The chapter's notes 10 and 16 cite *Keh Tong Chen v. Attorney General of the United States*, 546 F. Supp. 1060, 1064 (D.D.C. 1982), for the courts' refusal to find exceptional hardship in the ordinary distress of separation. The opinion itself couldn't be read on 2026-10-29: cite it only through the Policy Manual ("cited in 2 USCIS-PM D.4 n.16") until someone reads the pinpoint.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- *Mansour*, 11 I&N Dec. at 307, quotes a congressional report warning against leniency where a U.S. marriage or a child's birth is used to show hardship. Expect the officer to discount hardship that flows only from a family formed during the J program. Answer it with specific medical, financial, and country-condition evidence for both scenarios.
- *Mansour* is a District Director decision with no published citing decisions in ImmCite. Present it as the long-standing administrative standard, not as Board precedent.
- *Tayabji*, 19 I&N Dec. at 266: a waiver approved without a favorable State Department recommendation was invalid, and the adjustment that relied on it was rescinded.
