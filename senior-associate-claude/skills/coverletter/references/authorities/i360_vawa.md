## Propositions

**A1. An abused spouse or intended spouse of a U.S. citizen or LPR, an abused child of either, or an abused parent of a U.S. citizen 21 or older may self-petition and include their children; the self-petitioner must qualify as an immediate relative or under F2A.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(I), 8 U.S.C. § 1154: "may file a petition ... for classification of the alien (and any child of the alien)"; (iii)(II)(cc): "eligible to be classified as an immediate relative under section 1151(b)(2)(A)(i)". Children (A)(iv), (B)(iii); parents (A)(vii); LPR spouses (B)(ii); filers abroad (A)(v), (B)(iv). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(c)(1)(i) (spouse), (e)(1)(i) (child): "A child may file a self-petition under section 204(a)(1)(A)(iv) or 204(a)(1)(B)(iii) of the Act". Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. Battery or extreme cruelty by the abuser during the relationship includes threatened violence, psychological or sexual abuse, and acts that are part of a pattern of violence.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(I)(bb): "the alien or a child of the alien has been battered or has been the subject of extreme cruelty". Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(c)(1)(vi): "any act or threatened act of violence ... which results or threatens to result in physical or mental injury"; also acts "that are a part of an overall pattern of violence". Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E): the evidence should be "detailed, specific, and reliable". PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.
- *Hernandez v. Ashcroft*, 345 F.3d 824 (9th Cir. 2003) (doc 8278) (opening summary; connector text has no reporter pages, so confirm the pin): "an abuser's behavior during the 'contrite' phase of domestic violence may ... constitute 'extreme cruelty.'" (https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/783767/laura-luis-hernandez-v-john-ashcroft-attorney-general/). ImmCite caution; Ninth Circuit only (see adverse list). Checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. The self-petitioner must have lived with the abuser at some point, for no minimum period, possibly abroad.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(dd): "who has resided with the alien's spouse or intended spouse"; (A)(iv) (child): "residence includes any period of visitation". Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 3 USCIS-PM D.2(F): "not required ... to have resided with the abuser for any specific length of time or to have resided with the abuser in the United States". PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. Good faith turns on intent to build a life together at the marriage; separation or a failed marriage doesn't defeat it.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(I)(aa): "the marriage or the intent to marry the United States citizen was entered into in good faith by the alien". Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(c)(1)(ix): "A self-petition will not be denied, however, solely because the spouses are not living together and the marriage is no longer viable." Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- *Matter of Laureano*, 19 I&N Dec. 1, 2–3 (BIA 1983) (doc 898): "The central question is whether the bride and groom intended to establish a life together at the time they were married." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/2951.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 28 of 28 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. Good moral character is judged under INA 101(f), generally over the three years before filing; a waivable act or conviction connected to the abuse need not bar it.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(C): such an act or conviction doesn't bar the finding if it "was connected to the alien's having been battered or subjected to extreme cruelty". Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(c)(1)(vii): lacks good moral character "if he or she is a person described in section 101(f) of the Act"; (e)(2)(v): "A child who is less than 14 years of age is presumed to be a person of good moral character". Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 3 USCIS-PM D.2(G)(1): "USCIS generally, but not exclusively, looks at the 3-year period immediately preceding the date the self-petition is filed". PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. A bigamy-invalid marriage qualifies, and a former spouse may file within two years of the abuser's death (citizen abuser only), domestic-violence-related loss of status, or a divorce connected to the abuse; later changes don't defeat a filed petition.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(iii)(II)(aa)(BB): "not legitimate solely because of the bigamy of such citizen"; (CC): "a connection between the legal termination of the marriage within the past 2 years and battering or extreme cruelty". (B)(ii)(II)(aa)(CC) omits death for LPR abusers. Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(vi): "death of the abuser, divorce, or changes to the abuser's citizenship status after filing of the petition shall not adversely affect the approval"; (B)(v)(II): an LPR abuser's naturalization means the petition "shall be deemed reclassified as a petition filed under subparagraph (A)". Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 3 USCIS-PM D.3(A)(1): "The specific legal ground for a divorce or annulment does not need to be abuse." PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A7. USCIS considers any credible evidence; the burden is a preponderance.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(J): "shall consider any credible evidence relevant to the petition"; credibility and weight are "within the sole discretion" of the agency. 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(c)(2)(i) repeats it. Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 3 USCIS-PM D.2(A): eligibility is shown "by a preponderance of the evidence". PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A8. A self-petitioner with a citizen abuser adjusts as an immediate relative and may file the I-485 with or after the I-360.**
- INA § 204(a)(1)(A)(vi): changes after filing don't "preclude the classification ... as an immediate relative or affect the alien's ability to adjust status under subsections (a) and (c)" of § 1255. Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- USCIS, *Green Card for VAWA Self-Petitioner* (last updated 2026-06-26): an immediate relative "may file Form I-485 at any time because visas are always immediately available for immediate relatives" (https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-eligibility/green-card-for-vawa-self-petitioner). Checked 2026-10-28.

**A9. Inspection and admission aren't required, and none of the 245(c) bars applies.**
- INA § 245(a), (c), 8 U.S.C. § 1255: (a) reaches "any other alien having an approved petition for classification as a VAWA self-petitioner"; (c) opens "Other than an alien having an approved petition for classification as a VAWA self-petitioner" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1255/). INA § 101(a)(51) defines the term. Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 7 USCIS-PM B.2 (inspection): the requirement "does not apply to" ... "Violence Against Women Act (VAWA) applicants"; 7 USCIS-PM B.8(A): "All bars to adjustment do not apply to a battered or abused spouse, child, or parent of a U.S. citizen" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-b-chapter-8; B.2 is chapter-2). PM last updated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-28.

**A10. The public charge ground and the I-864 requirement don't apply.**
- INA § 212(a)(4)(E): "Subparagraphs (A), (B), and (C) shall not apply to an alien who— (i) is a VAWA self-petitioner" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1182/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 USCIS-PM G.3 (exempt categories): "Self-petitioners under the Violence Against Women Act (VAWA)" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-8-part-g-chapter-3). PM last updated 2026-10-27 (Part G revised 2026-09-18), checked 2026-10-28.

**A11. Background: DHS may not act solely on the abuser's information or disclose VAWA information.**
- 8 U.S.C. § 1367(a)(1)(A): no adverse determination "using information furnished solely by— (A) a spouse or parent who has battered the alien"; (a)(2) covers "any information which relates to an alien who is the beneficiary of an application for relief under paragraph ... (51)" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1367/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E): "Hurtful conduct alone, or adverse interactions of limited severity, are not sufficient to establish 'extreme' cruelty"; the acts "must reflect an intention to gain or maintain power and control". Prove the pattern of control in detail.
- *Hernandez* (A2) is ImmCite caution: its reviewability holding was rejected by the 2d, 3d, 5th, 7th, 10th and 11th Circuits and questioned in the Ninth (*Lopez-Birrueta*, 633 F.3d 1211); the contrite-phase holding still binds there. 3 USCIS-PM D.2(E): "USCIS agrees with the majority circuits courts' interpretations." Seven precedential citing decisions are unreviewed.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(c) predates VAWA 2000: it still lists extreme hardship, requires marriage at filing and past U.S. residence with the abuser, and bars automatic upgrade. 3 USCIS-PM D.1: VAWA 2000 "Removed the extreme hardship eligibility requirement" and the married-at-filing rule. Cite the statute; don't argue hardship as an element.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.2(c)(1)(ii): "The self-petitioner's remarriage, however, will be a basis for the denial of a pending self-petition." § 204.2(c)(1)(iv) applies INA 204(c), 204(g) and 204(a)(2).
- *Matter of Bador*, 28 I&N Dec. 638, 646 (BIA 2022) (doc 93): "A fraudulent marriage cannot be entered into in good faith" (https://www.justice.gov/d9/2024-02/4052_0.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 3 of 3 precedential citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; review denied, 107 F.4th 75 (2d Cir. 2024). Checked 2026-10-28.
- *Matter of Pangan-Sis*, 27 I&N Dec. 130, 135 (BIA 2017) (doc 4375): for the 212(a)(6)(A)(ii) exception, "an alien must satisfy subclauses (I), (II), and (III)," including a substantial connection between abuse and unlawful entry (https://www.justice.gov/media/917571/dl?inline). ImmCite treatment_available; 2 of 2 citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-28. A9 lifts the inspection requirement, not 212(a)(6)(A)(i).
- The 245(a) and (c) exceptions, 7 USCIS-PM B.8(A) and the USCIS page ("You must have an approved Form I-360 in order to qualify for a Green Card") need an approved I-360; F2A (LPR-abuser) cases also need an available visa.
- Adjustment is discretionary (PM-602-0199, May 21, 2026, flagged for 245(a); see `i130_i485.md`; date read on the memo 2026-10-29). The 7 USCIS-PM B alert (Aug. 13, 2026) implements *Matter of Delcarmen-Lara*, 29 I&N Dec. 830 (BIA 2026): advance-parole travel after 180+ days of unlawful presence can trigger 212(a)(9)(B).
