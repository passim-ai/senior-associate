## Propositions

**A1. EB-1A requires extraordinary ability shown by sustained national or international acclaim, continued work in the field, and prospective benefit to the United States.**
- INA § 203(b)(1)(A)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(1)(A)(i): "extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1153/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. EB-1A evidence is a one-time major international award or at least three of the ten regulatory criteria. Meeting three criteria is a threshold, followed by a final merits determination on the whole record.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(3): "evidence of a one-time achievement (that is, a major, international recognized award), or at least three of the following" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-204.5). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.2(B)(2): "In the second step of the analysis, the officer should consider the petition in its entirety to determine eligibility according to the standard." (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-f-chapter-2). PM last updated 2026-10-27; checked 2026-10-28.
- *Kazarian v. USCIS*, 596 F.3d 1115, 1121 (9th Cir. 2010) (doc 200254): "they are not relevant to the antecedent procedural question of whether the petitioner has provided at least three types of evidence" (https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1302728/kazarian-v-us-citizenship-immigration-services/). ImmCite treatment_available; all 3 published citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Binding only in the Ninth Circuit; cite the Policy Manual elsewhere. Checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. USCIS may waive the EB-2 job offer, and with it the labor certification, in the national interest.**
- INA § 203(b)(2)(B)(i), 8 U.S.C. § 1153(b)(2)(B)(i): "may, when the Attorney General deems it to be in the national interest, waive the requirements of subparagraph (A)" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1153/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. After EB-2 eligibility, a national interest waiver needs (1) an endeavor of substantial merit and national importance, (2) a petitioner well positioned to advance it, and (3) a balance that favors waiving the job offer.**
- *Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. 884, 889 (AAO 2016) (doc 118): "the foreign national's proposed endeavor has both substantial merit and national importance; (2) that the foreign national is well positioned to advance the proposed endeavor" (https://www.justice.gov/media/871246/dl?inline). ImmCite treatment_available; all 4 published citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; followed in *Poursina v. USCIS*, 936 F.3d 868 (9th Cir. 2019). Checked 2026-10-28.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D) & n.88, quoting *Dhanasar* at 889: "the potential to create a significant economic impact may be favorable but is not required" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-f-chapter-5). PM last updated 2026-10-27; checked 2026-10-28.

**A5. A job-offer petition must show the employer's ability to pay the proffered wage from the priority date until the beneficiary becomes a permanent resident.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(g)(2): "The petitioner must demonstrate this ability at the time the priority date is established and continuing until the beneficiary obtains lawful permanent residence." (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-204.5). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A6. The petitioner must prove eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence, meaning the claim is probably true.**
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): "requires that the evidence demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; all 12 published citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; followed in *Matter of Texperts, Inc.*, 29 I&N Dec. 491, 493 (AAO 2026) (re-checked at the source 2026-10-29). Checked 2026-10-28.

**A7. Adjustment requires inspection and admission or parole, eligibility for an immigrant visa, admissibility, and a visa immediately available when the I-485 is filed (the Visa Bulletin governs preference cases).**
- INA § 245(a), 8 U.S.C. § 1255(a): "the alien is eligible to receive an immigrant visa and is admissible to the United States for permanent residence" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1255/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 245.1(g)(1): "unless an immigrant visa is immediately available to him or her at the time the application is filed" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-245.1). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A8. An EB-1, EB-2, or EB-3 applicant who is present on a lawful admission can adjust despite the 245(c)(2), (c)(7) and (c)(8) bars if status violations total 180 days or less.**
- INA § 245(k)(2), 8 U.S.C. § 1255(k)(2): "has not, for an aggregate period exceeding 180 days— (A) failed to maintain, continuously, a lawful status; (B) engaged in unauthorized employment" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1255/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A9. Once the I-485 has been pending 180 days, the I-140 stays valid for a new job in the same or a similar occupational classification.**
- INA § 204(j), 8 U.S.C. § 1154(j): "shall remain valid with respect to a new job ... if the new job is in the same or a similar occupational classification" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1154/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- USCIS, PM-602-0199, *Adjustment of Status is a Matter of Discretion and Administrative Grace* (May 21, 2026), p. 5: "The absence of adverse factors, by itself, does not demonstrate such unusual or outstanding equities." It applies where consular processing is available, so argue affirmative equities rather than the absence of negatives, and check for a rescission before filing.
- *Kazarian* binds only in the Ninth Circuit. Outside it, rest the two-step review on 6 USCIS-PM F.2(B).
