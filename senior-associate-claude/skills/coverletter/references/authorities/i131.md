# Standard authorities: I-131 travel document (`i131`)

## Propositions

**A1. Parole, including advance parole, is a case-by-case discretionary grant for urgent humanitarian reasons or significant public benefit, and it is not an admission.**
- INA § 212(d)(5)(A), 8 U.S.C. § 1182(d)(5)(A): "only on a case-by-case basis for urgent humanitarian reasons or significant public benefit"; parole "shall not be regarded as an admission of the alien" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1182/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 212.5(f): when parole is authorized for travel to the United States without a visa, "the alien shall be issued an appropriate document authorizing travel" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-212.5). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A2. An adjustment applicant who travels without advance parole abandons the I-485, unless an exception applies.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 245.2(a)(4)(ii)(A): "the departure of an applicant who is not under exclusion, deportation, or removal proceedings shall be deemed an abandonment of the application" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-245.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 245.2(a)(4)(ii)(B)-(C): travel "shall not be deemed an abandonment of the application if he or she was previously granted advance parole," and (C) protects H-1/L-1, H-4/L-2 and K-3/K-4 applicants who return in status with a valid visa. Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A3. A permanent resident who plans a temporary absence may apply, from inside the United States, for a reentry permit, which replaces the returning resident visa.**
- INA § 223(a), 8 U.S.C. § 1203(a): a permanent resident "who intends to depart temporarily from the United States may make application ... for a permit to reenter" (https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t8/s1203/). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 223.1(a): the permit allows admission "upon return from abroad during the period of the permit's validity without the necessity of obtaining a returning resident visa" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-223.1). Current, checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 223.2(b)(1): the applicant "must file such application while in the United States and in status as a lawful permanent resident or conditional permanent resident" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-223.2). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

**A4. A refugee or asylee needs a refugee travel document, or valid advance parole, to return from temporary travel.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 223.1(b): such a person "must have a refugee travel document to return to the United States after temporary travel abroad unless ... in possession of a valid advance parole document" (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-223.1). Current, checked 2026-10-28.

## Adverse or limiting authority
- *Matter of Delcarmen-Lara*, 29 I&N Dec. 830, 830 (BIA 2026) (syllabus) (doc 516231): "A departure from the United States pursuant to a grant of advance parole is a 'departure' within the meaning of section 212(a)(9)(B)(i)(II)." ImmCite unchecked: no published decision cites it yet, which is not adverse. Advance-parole travel after more than 180 days of unlawful presence can now trigger the 3-year or 10-year bar. Screen unlawful presence before requesting advance parole, and never describe the trip as safe.
- *Matter of Arrabally and Yerrabelly*, 25 I&N Dec. 771 (BIA 2012) (doc 201564): ImmCite negative. *Delcarmen-Lara*, decided Aug. 13, 2026, overruled it (29 I&N Dec. at 835) and applies its holding prospectively (at 836), without saying which trips that covers (read at the source 2026-10-29). Don't cite *Arrabally* for future travel, and research a trip taken before Aug. 13, 2026 per client instead of assuming *Arrabally* still governs it.
- 3 USCIS-PM B.12 and the 7 USCIS-PM B part page carry alerts dated Aug. 13, 2026 implementing *Delcarmen-Lara*: "This decision has an immediate impact for certain aliens with valid Advance Parole Documents (Form I-512L)" (https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-3-part-b-chapter-12). Checked 2026-10-28.
- 8 C.F.R. § 245.2(a)(4)(ii)(B): if the I-485 is later denied, the returning traveler "will be treated as an applicant for admission."
