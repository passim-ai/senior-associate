## Propositions

**A1. EB-2 covers members of the professions holding advanced degrees or their equivalent, and aliens of exceptional ability. The NIW is available only within EB-2, so EB-2 eligibility is decided first.**
- INA § 203(b)(2)(A): "members of the professions holding advanced degrees or their equivalent or who because of their exceptional ability in the sciences, arts, or business". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(1): "If the person does not have the qualifications for the EB-2 classification, the petition is statutorily ineligible for the national interest waiver." Checked 2026-10-30.

**A2. An advanced degree is a degree above the baccalaureate, or a U.S. or foreign-equivalent bachelor's degree followed by five years of progressive experience in the specialty. The initial evidence is an official academic record, plus employer letters for the experience. A profession is an INA 101(a)(32) occupation or one that requires a bachelor's degree for entry.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(k)(2): "followed by at least five years of progressive experience in the specialty shall be considered the equivalent of a master's degree". "Profession" is defined in the same paragraph. Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(k)(3)(i)(A)–(B); (B): "letters from current or former employer(s) showing that the alien has at least five years of progressive post-baccalaureate experience in the specialty". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(g)(1): experience letters "shall include the name, address, and title of the writer, and a specific description of the duties performed by the alien". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(1): the experience "must occur after completion of the bachelor's degree", and "The occupation underlying the endeavor is determinative" of whether the person is a professional. Checked 2026-10-30.

**A3. Exceptional ability means expertise significantly above that ordinarily encountered. It requires at least three of six criteria (or comparable evidence), followed by a final merits determination. For an NIW, the area of ability must relate directly to the endeavor.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(k)(2): "a degree of expertise significantly above that ordinarily encountered in the sciences, arts, or business". (k)(3)(ii)(A)–(F) list the six criteria; (k)(3)(iii) allows "comparable evidence". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(B)(2) sets out a two-step review: Step 1 checks the regulatory criteria; Step 2 is a "Final merits determination". F.5(D)(1): "Meeting at least three criteria, however, does not, in and of itself, establish eligibility". The "area of exceptional ability" must be "directly related to the person's proposed endeavor." Checked 2026-10-30.

**A4. USCIS may waive the job offer, and with it the labor certification, in the national interest. Anyone may file. The petition must include the employee-specific portion of a labor certification: USCIS's filing guidance asks for Form ETA-9089 Appendix A and a signed ETA-9089 Final Determination, and the Policy Manual accepts either an ETA 750B or an ETA 9089.**
- INA § 203(b)(2)(B)(i): "may, when the Attorney General deems it to be in the national interest, waive the requirements of subparagraph (A)". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(k)(1): for the exemption, "the alien, or anyone in the alien's behalf, may be the petitioner". (k)(4)(ii) still requires "Form ETA-750B, Statement of Qualifications of Alien, in duplicate". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D) (this controls the form): "the petitioner must submit the employee-specific portions of a permanent labor certification (without DOL approval)"; "The petitioner may submit either the Form ETA 750B or Form ETA 9089." Checked 2026-10-30. The Form I-140 instructions (06/07/24) name no labor form for an NIW (p. 6, item 4.A; see A6) and nowhere mention "Appendix A".
- USCIS, *Employment-Based Immigration: Second Preference EB-2* (https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/permanent-workers/employment-based-immigration-second-preference-eb-2, updated 01/15/2025): "the petition must be accompanied by a completed Form ETA-9089, Appendix A and a signed Form ETA-9089, Final Determination." USCIS's I-140 initial evidence checklist (updated 11/06/2024) adds that the Final Determination "must be signed by the employer/petitioner, the attorney/agent (if applicable), and the beneficiary/self-petitioner." Filing both also meets the Policy Manual's "Form ETA 9089". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A5. The *Dhanasar* test replaced *NYSDOT*. Once EB-2 eligibility is shown, the petitioner must prove by a preponderance that (1) the endeavor has substantial merit and national importance, (2) the person is well positioned to advance it, and (3) on balance, waiving the job offer benefits the United States. The grant remains discretionary.**
- *Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. 884, 889 (AAO 2016) (doc 118): "(1) that the foreign national's proposed endeavor has both substantial merit and national importance; (2) that the foreign national is well positioned to advance the proposed endeavor" (https://www.justice.gov/media/871246/dl?inline). At 888: "Today, we vacate NYSDOT and adopt a new framework". At 889 n.9: the waiver is "purely discretionary". ImmCite treatment_available; 4 of 4 published citing decisions reviewed, none adverse; followed in *Poursina* (A11). Checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(2): "USCIS may grant a national interest waiver as a matter of discretion" if eligibility is shown "by a preponderance of the evidence, based on the following three prongs". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A6. Prong 1 (merit and importance). Merit can lie in any field, with or without economic impact. National importance turns on the endeavor's potential prospective impact and broader implications, not its geographic reach. The endeavor must be specific, and benefit to one employer or its clients alone is not enough.**
- *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 889: "The endeavor's merit may be demonstrated in a range of areas such as business, entrepreneurialism, science, technology, culture, health, or education." Also at 889: "we consider its potential prospective impact"; "we do not evaluate prospective impact solely in geographic terms. Instead, we look for broader implications." At 890: "significant potential to employ U.S. workers ... particularly in an economically depressed area". Checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(3) (First Prong): "The term 'endeavor' is more specific than the general occupation". It also says: "Benefits to a specific employer alone, even an employer with a national footprint, are not sufficiently relevant". And: the person's endeavor must stand "to have broader implications, such as for a field, a region, or the public at large." Checked 2026-10-30.
- Form I-140 Instructions (06/07/24), p. 6, item 4.A: a waiver request must come "with documentation provided showing the alien's presence in the United States is in the national interest". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A7. Prong 2 (well positioned). The factors are education, skills, knowledge and record of success; a model or plan; progress; and interest from customers, users, investors or others. The petitioner need not show the endeavor is more likely than not to succeed.**
- *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 890: "a model or plan for future activities; any progress towards achieving the proposed endeavor; and the interest of potential customers, users, investors, or other relevant entities". Also at 890: "We do not, therefore, require petitioners to demonstrate that their endeavors are more likely than not to ultimately succeed." Checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(3) (Second Prong) lists types of evidence ("not meant to be a checklist") but warns: "unsubstantiated claims would not meet the petitioner's burden of proof." Business plans "should be supported by other independent evidence." Checked 2026-10-30.

**A8. Prong 3 (balance). The factors include whether a labor certification is impractical, the benefit even if qualified U.S. workers are available, urgency, unique skills a labor certification cannot capture, economic impact, and job creation. Self-employed entrepreneurs may gain little from a labor certification.**
- *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 890: whether "it would be impractical either for the foreign national to secure a job offer or for the petitioner to obtain a labor certification". At 891: "even assuming that other qualified U.S. workers are available, the United States would still benefit". Also at 891: whether the interest "is sufficiently urgent to warrant forgoing the labor certification process". At 890–91 n.10, on entrepreneurs and the self-employed: it may be impractical for a "self-employed inventor ... to secure a job offer from a U.S. employer." Checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(3) (Third Prong): where "the person is self-employed in a manner that generally does not adversely affect U.S. workers ... there may be little benefit from the labor certification". The Third Prong list also includes potential job creation and "considerable economic impact". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A9. Advanced STEM degrees and critical and emerging technologies. A STEM Ph.D. tied to the endeavor is "an especially positive factor" under prong 2. An advanced STEM degree, plus work on a critical and emerging technology, plus being well positioned, is "a strong positive factor" under prong 3. Letters from interested government agencies help but are not required.**
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(4): such a degree is "an especially positive factor to be considered along with other evidence". Also: "A degree in and of itself, however, is not a sufficient basis". F.5(D)(5): "While not required, letters from interested government agencies or quasi-governmental entities ... can be helpful evidence". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A10. Entrepreneurs may qualify through a U.S. entity they own and run. Officers weigh ownership, growth, investment, and third-party letters in the aggregate. General claims about the economy or job creation are not enough.**
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(6): "broad assertions regarding general benefits to the economy and potential to create jobs will not establish an entrepreneur's qualification". Ownership "rarely establishes eligibility on its own". Letters from third-party reviewers "may have probative value". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A11. The NIW decision is discretionary, and courts lack jurisdiction to review a denial.**
- *Poursina v. USCIS*, 936 F.3d 868, 871 (9th Cir. 2019) (doc 205397): "Congress's use of 'deems' connotes that the Attorney General's determination involves some measure of judgment." (https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4656790/mohammad-poursina-v-uscis/). The pin comes from *Chairez v. Mayorkas*, 168 F.4th 1227 (9th Cir. 2026), which cites "936 F.3d at 871"; the connector text has no F.3d page breaks. ImmCite caution: *Chairez* rejected a strand of its reasoning, but its holdings stand (NIW denials are unreviewable under § 1252(a)(2)(B)(ii); colorable constitutional claims remain reviewable). 9 of 9 published citing decisions reviewed. Checked 2026-10-30.

**A12. The petitioner must prove eligibility by a preponderance of the evidence, meaning the claim is probably true.**
- *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 376 (AAO 2010) (doc 300): "requires that the evidence demonstrate that the applicant's claim is 'probably true'" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol25/3700.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 12 of 12 published citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30.

**A13. Expert and support letters are advisory. Their weight depends on the writer's first-hand knowledge, specific examples, and corroboration.**
- *Matter of Caron International, Inc.*, 19 I&N Dec. 791, 795 (Comm'r 1988) (doc 1032): expert opinions are "advisory opinions"; where one "is not in accord with other information or is in any way questionable, the Service is not required to accept" it (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol19/3085.pdf). The page was read on the scanned PDF. ImmCite treatment_available; 2 of 2 published citing decisions reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30.
- 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(3) (Second Prong): letters "may be persuasive when they are from experts in the person's field who have first-hand knowledge" and "are supported by other independent evidence". Checked 2026-10-30.

**A14. Without a job offer, the regulation's ability-to-pay duty does not attach by its terms.** State no more than the sources do: neither source says "an NIW needs no ability-to-pay evidence," and F.5 does not address the point.
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(g)(2): the duty applies to "Any petition filed by or for an employment-based immigrant which requires an offer of employment". Current, checked 2026-10-30.
- Form I-140 Instructions (06/07/24), p. 8, General Evidence item 2: "You must submit evidence with petitions requiring job offers that the prospective U.S. employer has the ability to pay the offered wage." Checked 2026-10-30.

**A15. Without a labor certification, the priority date is the date the complete, signed petition is properly filed with USCIS.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(d): "which does not require a labor certification ... shall be the date the completed, signed petition (including all initial evidence and the correct fee) is properly filed with USCIS". Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A16. An accompanying or following-to-join spouse or child takes the same status and order of consideration.**
- INA § 203(d): "be entitled to the same status, and the same order of consideration provided in the respective subsection, if accompanying or following to join". Current, checked 2026-10-30.

**A17. Premium processing for an NIW I-140 is 45 business days. The clock starts only when all prerequisites are received, and it restarts after an RFE or NOID response.**
- 8 C.F.R. § 106.4(e)(18): "involving a waiver under section 203(b)(2)(B) of the Act: 45 business days". (f)(2)(ii) and (f)(3) govern the start and the restart (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-106.4). Current, checked 2026-10-30. USCIS's premium processing page (updated 03/23/2026) agrees: "45 business days for ... Form I-140 E21 national interest waiver". The fee is $2,965 (G-1055, ed. 10/26/26, p. 28; 8 C.F.R. § 106.4(c)(18)).

## Adverse or limiting authority
- **Eligibility at filing; no material change.** *Matter of Katigbak*, 14 I&N Dec. 45, 49 (Reg'l Comm'r 1971) (doc 1703): "A petition may not be approved for a profession for which the beneficiary is not qualified at the time of its filing." (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol14/2125.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 6 of 6 reviewed, none adverse. *Matter of Izummi*, 22 I&N Dec. 169, 175 (Assoc. Comm'r 1998) (doc 638): "may not make material changes to a petition that has already been filed in an effort to make an apparently deficient petition conform" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol22/3360.pdf). ImmCite caution: *Chang v. United States*, 327 F.3d 911 (9th Cir. 2003), limits its retroactive use only for certain EB-5 I-829 petitions; its material-change rule still stands. 5 of 5 reviewed. Checked 2026-10-30. Define the endeavor fully at filing, because an RFE response that redefines it invites a *Izummi* finding.
- **Three-year degrees.** *Matter of Shah*, 17 I&N Dec. 244, 245 (Reg'l Comm'r 1977) (doc 1193): "a 3-year course of study, which is not equivalent to a United States baccalaureate degree, usually requiring 4 years of study" (https://www.justice.gov/eoir/vll/intdec/vol17/2757.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 1 of 1 reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30. 6 USCIS-PM F.5(A)(2) requires "a foreign degree evaluated to be the equivalent of a U.S. bachelor's degree", and a beneficiary without one "is ineligible for this classification". The chapter's only "single" language (F.5(A)(3)) concerns labor-certification job requirements ("comparable to a single U.S. bachelor's degree"). Do not cite F.5 for a general single-degree rule; research combined credentials per client.
- **Weak endeavors named in the Policy Manual.** 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(3) says the following generally fall short: classroom teaching "without broader implications for a field or region"; "Proposing to work in an occupation with a national shortage ... alone"; "the general importance of business owners or entrepreneurs for job creation"; and a software engineer adapting an employer's code for clients. Under the Third Prong, "evidence of a national labor shortage in the person's occupation would not, by itself, satisfy this third prong." Checked 2026-10-30.
- **Letters.** 6 USCIS-PM F.5(B)(2): contemporaneous recognition "may have more weight than letters prepared for the petition". See also *Caron* (A13). Corroborate every letter.
- **Discretion and review.** *Flores v. Garland*, 72 F.4th 85 (5th Cir. 2023) (doc 4880), slip op. at 12 (F.4th page not verified): "Dhanasar embraces agency discretion to deny national-interest waivers." (http://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/22/22-20419-CV0.pdf). ImmCite treatment_available; 4 of 4 reviewed, none adverse. Checked 2026-10-30. With *Poursina* (A11), this means a denial generally cannot be taken to court, so the record has to win at USCIS or the AAO.
- **Athletes.** 6 USCIS-PM F.5(D)(3): no NIW "for male athletes whose proposed endeavor is to compete in women's sports" (added 2025-08-04 per the update history).
- **Do not cite *NYSDOT*.** *Matter of New York State Dep't of Transp.*, 22 I&N Dec. 215 (Acting Assoc. Comm'r 1998) (doc 635) is ImmCite "vacated" by *Dhanasar*, and ImmCite says it has "no precedential force."
